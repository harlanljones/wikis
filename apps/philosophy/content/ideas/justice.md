# Justice

Justice is about giving each person what they are due. The old formula is easy to state and settles nothing, because the whole argument is over what people are due and why: what they deserve, what they need, what they have a right to, or what a fair agreement would give them.

## Kinds of justice

[[thinkers/aristotle|Aristotle's]] division is still used.

- **Distributive justice** concerns how the benefits and burdens of social life are shared out: wealth, opportunity, office, taxes.
- **Corrective justice** concerns putting right a wrong between two parties, by compensation or punishment.

To these are usually added **procedural justice**, the fairness of the process by which decisions are made, whatever the result, and **retributive justice**, the question of what punishment wrongdoers deserve.

Most modern debate is about distributive justice, often called **social justice**. It became pressing once people came to see the distribution of wealth as something societies create through their laws, and could change.

### The ancient view

For [[thinkers/plato|Plato]], justice is first a condition of the soul, a kind of inner health, and a just city mirrors it. For [[thinkers/aristotle|Aristotle]], justice in distribution is proportional: equals should get equal shares and unequals unequal ones. He saw the difficulty at once. Everyone agrees that shares should follow merit, but democrats take merit to be free birth, oligarchs wealth and aristocrats virtue.

### Utility

[[traditions/utilitarianism|Utilitarians]] hold that institutions should be arranged to maximise welfare. Because an extra dollar means more to a poor person than a rich one, this tends to favour some redistribution. The standing objection is that it would allow the few to be sacrificed if the total gain were large enough.

### Rawls and his critics

[[thinkers/rawls|Rawls]] set out to give an alternative. Behind a veil of ignorance, people would choose equal basic liberties, fair equality of opportunity, and inequalities only where they benefit the worst off. Since 1971 most theories of justice have defined themselves by where they part from him.

- [[thinkers/nozick|Nozick]] denies that justice is about the pattern of who has what. It is about history: whether people got what they have by legitimate steps. See [[debates/rawls-vs-nozick|Rawls vs Nozick]].
- [[thinkers/hayek|Hayek]] denies that a market distribution can be just or unjust at all, since no one decides it.
- **Luck egalitarians**, such as Ronald Dworkin and G. A. Cohen, hold that inequalities are unjust when they come from bad luck and acceptable when they come from choices people are responsible for.
- **Michael Walzer**, in *Spheres of Justice* (1983), argues that different goods should be distributed by different rules. Money should not buy votes, medical care or love. Injustice is one sphere's good dominating the others.
- [[thinkers/sen|Sen]] argues that we should compare real situations and remove clear injustices, without waiting for a theory of the perfectly just society.

## Wider questions

Recent work asks what theories of distribution leave out.

- **Recognition.** Iris Marion Young and Nancy Fraser argue that injustice includes disrespect, stereotyping and exclusion, which are not matters of who has how much.
- **The family.** Susan Moller Okin argued that the division of labour at home is a question of justice that theorists had treated as private. See [[traditions/feminism|feminism]].
- **Global justice.** Do principles of justice stop at borders? Some hold that they apply to the whole world. Others, including Rawls himself, hold that they apply within societies and that weaker duties hold between them.
- **Future generations** and **animals**, who cannot be parties to any agreement.
- **Historical injustice.** What is owed for slavery, conquest and colonial rule?

## The sceptics

In Plato's *Republic*, Thrasymachus says that justice is simply the advantage of the stronger. [[thinkers/marx|Marx]] thought that each society's idea of justice reflects its economic system, and [[thinkers/nietzsche|Nietzsche]] that talk of justice often masks resentment. Every theory of justice has to explain why it is not just one more case of what they describe.
