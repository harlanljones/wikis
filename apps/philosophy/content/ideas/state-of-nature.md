# The state of nature

The state of nature is a picture of human life without government. Philosophers use it to show what government is for: whatever problems would exist without it are the problems it exists to solve.

## A thought experiment, mostly

Few of the writers who used the idea thought they were writing history. [[thinkers/hobbes|Hobbes]] admitted there may never have been a time when the whole world lived that way, though he pointed to civil wars and to the relations between states. [[thinkers/rousseau|Rousseau]] began by "setting aside all the facts." The state of nature is a way of taking society apart in thought to see what each piece does.

It does contain a theory of human nature, and that is where the versions differ. How one imagines people without government decides what kind of government one thinks they need.

## Three pictures

| | [[thinkers/hobbes\|Hobbes]] | [[thinkers/locke\|Locke]] | [[thinkers/rousseau\|Rousseau]] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| People are | Equal, fearful, self-seeking | Equal, rational, bound by a moral law | Solitary, simple, capable of pity |
| Life is | "Solitary, poor, nasty, brutish, and short" | Mostly peaceful, but insecure | Peaceful and content, before society |
| Is there morality? | No justice or injustice without a common power | Yes. The law of nature binds everyone | No morality yet, but no vice either |
| Is there property? | No. Only what one can take and hold | Yes, acquired by labour | No. Property is the beginning of the trouble |
| What goes wrong | War of all against all | Everyone is judge in their own case | Society itself, comparison and inequality |
| So government must | Hold overwhelming power | Be a limited, impartial judge | Be remade so that citizens rule themselves |

### Hobbes

Three things cause conflict among equals: competition, distrust and glory. Even peaceful people must attack first, since they cannot be sure of others. The modern way of putting this is as a **prisoner's dilemma**. Each person does best by arming, whatever the others do, and all end up worse off than if they had cooperated. Only an outside enforcer changes the calculation.

### Locke

Locke separates the state of nature from the state of war. People can live together "according to reason, without a common superior." They have rights, they own things, they trade. What they lack is "an established, settled, known law," an impartial judge and the power to enforce judgements. Government is a convenience, and so it can be held to conditions.

### Rousseau

Rousseau thought both had read the vices of society back into nature. Truly natural humans had few needs and little reason to fight. Pride, greed and domination came later, with settled life, comparison and above all private property. The state of nature is lost for good, but it shows that our present condition is not fixed by nature.

## Later uses

- [[thinkers/kant|Kant]] argued that even among perfectly well-meaning people rights would be uncertain without public law, so there is a duty to leave the state of nature.
- [[thinkers/rawls|Rawls's]] **original position** is a state of nature rebuilt as a fair setting for choosing principles. Its parties are not savages. They are citizens deprived of information about themselves.
- [[thinkers/nozick|Nozick]] started from Locke's state of nature to see whether a state could arise without violating anyone's rights.
- In international relations, [[traditions/realism|realists]] describe a world of states with no world government as a Hobbesian state of nature, usually called "anarchy".

## Objections

[[thinkers/aristotle|Aristotle]] would have rejected the whole exercise. If humans are political animals, there is no pre-political human to study. [[thinkers/hume|Hume]] called the state of nature "a mere philosophical fiction." Anthropology suggests that humans have always lived in groups with rules.

Feminist critics add that the grown, independent individuals of the state of nature seem to have sprung up "like mushrooms," in Hobbes's own phrase, with no one having borne or raised them. The picture hides the family, and the work done in it.

The reply is the same as for the [[ideas/social-contract|social contract]]. The state of nature is not a claim about the past. It is a way of asking what we owe to political institutions and what they owe to us.
