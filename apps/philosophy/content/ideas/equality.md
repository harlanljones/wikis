# Equality

Equality in political thought is the claim that people should be treated as equals, and the long argument about what that requires. Almost every modern theory accepts that all persons have equal moral worth. They differ over what should be made equal as a result.

## Moral equality

The starting point is a claim about status, not a description. People plainly differ in strength, talent and character. The claim is that none of these differences makes one person's life and interests count for more than another's.

The idea has several roots: the Stoic belief that all humans share in reason, the Christian teaching that all are equal before God, and [[thinkers/hobbes|Hobbes's]] blunt point that anyone can kill anyone. [[thinkers/locke|Locke]] held that people are "equal and independent." [[thinkers/kant|Kant]] gave it its strongest form. Every person has dignity, a worth beyond price.

For most of history the dominant view was the opposite. [[thinkers/plato|Plato]] and [[thinkers/aristotle|Aristotle]] took natural hierarchy for granted, and [[thinkers/nietzsche|Nietzsche]] attacked equality as the morality of the herd.

The philosopher Will Kymlicka has suggested that modern political theories all stand on an "egalitarian plateau." [[traditions/libertarianism|Libertarians]] show equal respect by giving everyone equal rights over themselves, [[traditions/utilitarianism|utilitarians]] by counting everyone's happiness equally, [[traditions/socialism|socialists]] by seeking equal conditions. The dispute is over how to interpret equal concern.

## Equality of what?

| Kind | Meaning | Typical supporters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal and political | The same laws, rights and votes for all | Nearly everyone today |
| Formal opportunity | Careers open to talents. No one barred by birth, race or sex | Classical [[traditions/liberalism\|liberals]] |
| Fair opportunity | Those with similar talents and effort should have similar prospects, whatever their background | [[thinkers/rawls\|Rawls]] |
| Outcome or condition | Roughly equal income, wealth or welfare | Many [[traditions/socialism\|socialists]] |
| Capability | Equal real freedom to live a life one has reason to value | [[thinkers/sen\|Sen]], [[thinkers/nussbaum\|Nussbaum]] |
| Social | A society without ranks, in which no one must bow to anyone | Relational egalitarians |

### Opportunity

Equal opportunity sounds modest and turns out to be demanding. Formal opportunity removes legal barriers but lets advantages pass from parents to children. Fair opportunity requires schooling, health care and limits on inherited wealth to make the starting line level. And if it is unfair for social background to decide one's fate, is it not equally unfair for inborn talent to do so? [[thinkers/rawls|Rawls]] thought so, which is why he added the difference principle.

### Luck and responsibility

**Luck egalitarianism**, associated with Ronald Dworkin and G. A. Cohen, makes this the centre of the theory. Inequalities that come from circumstances a person did not choose, such as family, genes or accident, are unjust. Those that come from their own free choices are not.

### Equality as a relationship

Elizabeth Anderson, in a 1999 article titled "What Is the Point of Equality?", objected that luck egalitarians had lost sight of what egalitarian movements fought for. The aim was never to compensate people for bad luck. It was to end oppression: relations of domination, exploitation and contempt. A society of equals is one in which people can look each other in the eye. This **relational** view connects with [[traditions/republicanism|republican]] freedom and with [[thinkers/rousseau|Rousseau's]] demand that no citizen be rich enough to buy another.

## Objections

- **Levelling down.** If equality matters in itself, then making the better off worse off, with no gain to anyone, would be in one way an improvement. Many find that absurd. One response is **prioritarianism**: what matters is helping the worse off, not equality as such. Another is **sufficiency**: what matters is that everyone has enough.
- **Liberty.** [[thinkers/nozick|Nozick]] and [[thinkers/hayek|Hayek]] argue that equal outcomes can only be kept up by constant interference with free choices. Egalitarians reply that poverty limits freedom too. See [[ideas/liberty|liberty]].
- **Incentives.** Unequal rewards may make everyone richer. [[thinkers/rawls|Rawls]] accepts this, within limits.
- **Desert.** Some people work harder or contribute more and, on a common view, deserve more.

## Equality and difference

Treating people as equals does not always mean treating them the same. [[thinkers/marx|Marx]] noted that an equal standard applied to unequal people gives unequal results. [[traditions/feminism|Feminists]] have argued about whether equality means access to institutions designed around men or changing those institutions. Similar questions arise over disability, and over measures such as affirmative action that treat people differently in order to correct an inherited disadvantage.

[[thinkers/tocqueville|Tocqueville]] raised a different worry. The passion for equality may be so strong that people will accept equality in servitude rather than inequality in freedom.
