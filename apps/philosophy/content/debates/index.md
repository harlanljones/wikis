# Debates

Political philosophy moves forward by argument. These pages set out six of its best-known disputes, with each side's case in a table and the main replies.

They are a good way into the subject. Each can be read without having read anything else, and each links back to the thinkers involved.

| Debate | The question | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[debates/plato-vs-aristotle\|Plato vs Aristotle]] | Should politics start from an ideal or from experience? Should the wise rule? | Fourth century BC |
| [[debates/hobbes-locke-rousseau\|Hobbes, Locke and Rousseau]] | What would people in a state of nature agree to: an absolute ruler, a limited government or rule by all? | 1651 to 1762 |
| [[debates/burke-vs-paine\|Burke vs Paine]] | Does a people inherit its constitution or have the right to remake it? | 1790 to 1792 |
| [[debates/harm-principle\|The harm principle]] | May the law forbid what harms no one but the person doing it? | 1859 to today |
| [[debates/markets-vs-planning\|Markets vs planning]] | Can an economy be run from the centre? | 1920 to 1945, and after |
| [[debates/rawls-vs-nozick\|Rawls vs Nozick]] | Does justice require redistribution or forbid it? | 1971 to 1974 |
