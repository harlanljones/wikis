# Conservatism

Conservatism is the political tradition that values inherited institutions and customs, distrusts plans to remake society from abstract principles, and prefers gradual change to revolution. It is less a doctrine than a disposition, and conservatives in different countries have defended very different things.

## Core themes

- **The limits of reason.** Society is too complex for any one mind or generation to understand fully. Institutions that have lasted hold the lessons of long experience, even when no one can state the reasons for them.
- **Human imperfection.** People are fallible and often selfish. Good order depends on habit, restraint and authority, not on a hoped-for change in human nature.
- **Society as an organism.** A society is not a contract among individuals. It grows, and it joins the dead, the living and the unborn.
- **Reform, not revolution.** Change is necessary and should repair what exists. [[thinkers/burke|Burke]]: "A state without the means of some change is without the means of its conservation."
- **Little platoons.** Family, church, neighbourhood and local association come before the state and should be protected from it.
- **Order before liberty.** Freedom is possible only within a settled order, and is one good among others.

## A short history

**Ancestors.** [[thinkers/aristotle|Aristotle's]] preference for practical wisdom over theory, [[thinkers/augustine|Augustine's]] view of fallen human nature and [[thinkers/hume|Hume's]] respect for custom all feed the tradition.

**Burke.** Modern conservatism begins with [[thinkers/burke|Burke's]] *Reflections on the Revolution in France* (1790). The revolutionaries, he argued, were destroying a living society for the sake of a theory, and would end in terror and military rule. The word "conservative" came into political use a generation later.

**The Continental right.** In France and elsewhere a harder line took shape. Joseph de Maistre defended throne, altar and the executioner as the foundations of order and rejected the Enlightenment whole. This reactionary tradition wanted to reverse the Revolution, not to manage change, and it is the ancestor of the authoritarian right. [[thinkers/schmitt|Carl Schmitt]] belongs to it.

**Britain and America.** British conservatism, from Benjamin Disraeli's "one nation" politics on, accepted democracy and social reform as ways of preserving the social fabric. American conservatism had no throne or aristocracy to defend and has mostly sought to conserve a liberal constitution, religious faith and free enterprise. Russell Kirk's *The Conservative Mind* (1953) gave it a Burkean pedigree.

**The twentieth century.** Michael Oakeshott gave the most refined statement of the disposition. To be conservative, he wrote, is "to prefer the familiar to the unknown, to prefer the tried to the untried, fact to mystery, the actual to the possible." He attacked "rationalism in politics," the belief that governing is a technique to be learned from a book. [[thinkers/hayek|Hayek]] made a parallel case from economics, though he refused the label. Roger Scruton restated the tradition for a later generation.

## Varieties

| Kind | Emphasis | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Traditionalist | Custom, religion, local community | [[thinkers/burke\|Burke]], Kirk, Scruton |
| Liberal or free-market | Property, enterprise, limited government | Thatcher, Reagan, drawing on [[thinkers/hayek\|Hayek]] |
| Paternalist | The duty of the fortunate to the poor | Disraeli, Christian democracy in Europe |
| Reactionary or authoritarian | Restoration of authority | de Maistre, [[thinkers/schmitt\|Schmitt]] |
| National or populist | The nation and its culture against elites and outsiders | Much of the current right |

These pull against each other. The market, which liberal conservatives prize, is among the most powerful solvents of tradition, as [[thinkers/marx|Marx]] noted with approval and traditionalists with dismay.

## Conservatism and its neighbours

Conservatives share with [[traditions/communitarianism|communitarians]] the view that people are formed by their communities, and with [[traditions/realism|realists]] a low estimate of what politics can achieve. [[thinkers/tocqueville|Tocqueville]], usually called a liberal, is read by conservatives for his defence of associations and religion.

## Objections

- **Which traditions?** Slavery, serfdom and the subjection of women were all old. Conservatism needs some standard beyond age for telling a valuable inheritance from an entrenched injustice, and it is not clear it has one. See [[debates/burke-vs-paine|Burke vs Paine]].
- **Whose interests?** Critics from Paine on have seen it as a defence of whoever currently holds property and power.
- **No fixed content.** What conservatives conserve changes. Today's conservatives defend arrangements that their predecessors resisted. Defenders reply that this is the point: it is a way of handling change, not a list of positions.
- **Status quo bias.** The fact that an institution has survived does not show that it works well for those who live under it.

## Start reading

[[thinkers/burke|Burke's]] *Reflections*, Oakeshott's essays "On Being Conservative" and "Rationalism in Politics," and [[thinkers/hayek|Hayek's]] "Why I Am Not a Conservative" for the view from next door.
