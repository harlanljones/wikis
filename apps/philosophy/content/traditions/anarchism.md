# Anarchism

Anarchism is the tradition that rejects the state, and coercive authority in general, as unnecessary and harmful. Anarchists hold that people can organise their common life through voluntary cooperation. The word means "without rulers," not "without order."

## Core ideas

- **The state is illegitimate.** No one has the right to command others, and governments rest finally on force.
- **Authority corrupts** both those who hold it and those who submit to it.
- **Order without rulers.** Much of social life already runs on custom, agreement and mutual aid. More of it could.
- **Means and ends must match.** A free society cannot be built by authoritarian methods. This is the root of the anarchists' quarrel with [[thinkers/marx|Marxism]].

Anarchism takes the starting point of the [[ideas/social-contract|social contract]] tradition, that authority needs justifying, and concludes that the justification fails. See [[ideas/authority-and-obligation|authority and obligation]].

## The main figures

**William Godwin** (1756–1836), husband of [[thinkers/wollstonecraft|Mary Wollstonecraft]], gave the first systematic statement in *An Enquiry Concerning Political Justice* (1793). Government stands in the way of individual judgement, and as people grow more rational it will wither.

**Pierre-Joseph Proudhon** (1809–1865) was the first to call himself an anarchist. His slogan "property is theft" attacked ownership that lets a person live off others' labour. He proposed **mutualism**: a society of independent producers and cooperatives exchanging on equal terms, with free credit from a people's bank.

**Mikhail Bakunin** (1814–1876), a Russian revolutionary, fought [[thinkers/marx|Marx]] for control of the First International and was expelled in 1872. He predicted that a "workers' state" run by a party of socialist experts would become a new despotism, "the reign of scientific intelligence, the most aristocratic, despotic, arrogant and contemptuous of all regimes." Many readers think the twentieth century proved him right.

**Peter Kropotkin** (1842–1921), a Russian prince and geographer, argued in *Mutual Aid* (1902) that cooperation is as much a factor in evolution as competition. He advocated **anarcho-communism**: common ownership and distribution according to need, organised by free communes.

**Emma Goldman** (1869–1940) linked anarchism to free speech, women's emancipation and sexual freedom, and was deported from the United States in 1919.

## Varieties

| Kind | Vision | Names |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mutualism | Markets among cooperatives and small producers, without capitalists | Proudhon |
| Collectivism and anarcho-communism | Common ownership, federated communes | Bakunin, Kropotkin |
| Anarcho-syndicalism | Trade unions as both the means of revolution and the framework of the new society | Rudolf Rocker, the Spanish CNT |
| Individualist anarchism | The sovereign individual, bound only by their own agreements | Max Stirner, Benjamin Tucker |
| Anarcho-capitalism | Private property and markets for everything, including law and defence | Murray Rothbard |
| Philosophical anarchism | No duty to obey the state, with no programme for abolishing it | Robert Paul Wolff, A. John Simmons |

Most anarchists are socialists and regard anarcho-capitalism as a branch of [[traditions/libertarianism|libertarianism]], since in their view the boss and the landlord rule as surely as the state does.

## In practice

Anarchism was a mass movement for a time. In Spain during the civil war that began in 1936, anarchist unions ran factories and farms collectively across much of Catalonia and Aragon, until they were crushed by both Franco and their Communist allies. George Orwell described it in *Homage to Catalonia*.

A wave of assassinations around 1900, called "propaganda of the deed," gave anarchism its lasting association with bombs, which most anarchists rejected.

Anarchist ideas appear in movements that organise without leaders and decide by assembly, and in the work of scholars such as James C. Scott, who studies how people evade and resist states, and David Graeber, who argued that human societies have organised themselves in far more varied ways than the usual story allows.

## Objections

- **Hobbes's problem.** Without an enforcer, the [[ideas/state-of-nature|state of nature]] returns. Anarchists reply that [[thinkers/hobbes|Hobbes]] took the product of a competitive, unequal society for human nature, and that the state itself is the greatest source of violence.
- **Scale.** Face-to-face cooperation works in small groups. Coordinating millions may need authority.
- **Private power.** Remove the state and the strong may simply rule directly. [[thinkers/nozick|Nozick]] argued that a dominant protection agency, in effect a state, would arise from anarchy without anyone intending it.
- **Public goods** such as defence and public health are hard to provide by voluntary agreement.

Even those unconvinced can take anarchism as a standing question to every government: what gives you the right?
