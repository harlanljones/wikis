# Traditions

Political thinkers are usually grouped into traditions: families of ideas that share a view of what matters most in politics. These pages describe ten of them, with their history, their internal divisions and the main objections to each.

The labels are loose. Most traditions were named after the fact, many thinkers fit more than one, and some, such as [[thinkers/nietzsche|Nietzsche]] and [[thinkers/arendt|Arendt]], fit none.

## How they relate

Three of these are the main ideologies of modern politics, born from the French and industrial revolutions: [[traditions/liberalism|liberalism]], [[traditions/conservatism|conservatism]] and [[traditions/socialism|socialism]]. Most political parties descend from one of them.

Others are best understood by what they react to.

- [[traditions/libertarianism|Libertarianism]] is liberalism's free-market wing taken to its limit. [[traditions/anarchism|Anarchism]] goes a step further and removes the state.
- [[traditions/communitarianism|Communitarianism]] and modern [[traditions/republicanism|republicanism]] are both criticisms of liberalism from close by.
- [[traditions/feminism|Feminism]] cuts across all the others. There are liberal, socialist and radical feminists.
- [[traditions/utilitarianism|Utilitarianism]] is a moral theory more than a political programme, and has been used to support liberal, socialist and conservative conclusions.
- [[traditions/realism|Realism]] is a way of approaching politics that can be combined with several of the rest.

## What is not here yet

This wiki centres on the Western canon. It does not yet have pages on nationalism, fascism, environmental political thought, or the Confucian, Islamic, Indian and African traditions of political philosophy. See [[about]] for the scope and the gaps.
