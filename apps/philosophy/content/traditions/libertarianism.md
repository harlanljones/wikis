# Libertarianism

Libertarianism is the view that individual liberty and private property are the highest political values, and that government should do little more than protect people against force and fraud. It extends the classical strand of liberalism to its limit.

In Europe the word "libertarian" once meant an anarchist of the left, and "libertarian socialism" still does. This page is about the free-market position that took the name in the United States in the mid-twentieth century.

## Core ideas

- **Self-ownership.** Each person owns their own body, labour and life.
- **Strong property rights.** What a person justly acquires or receives in free exchange is theirs.
- **The non-aggression principle.** No one may start the use of force against another person or their property. Force is legitimate only in defence.
- **Voluntary exchange.** Whatever arises from the free agreement of adults is permissible.
- **The minimal state.** Government should be limited to police, courts and defence. Taxation for other purposes takes what belongs to individuals.

In practice libertarians are economically on the right (low taxes, free trade, deregulation) and socially permissive (free speech, drug legalisation, open or freer migration, opposition to war and to the surveillance state).

## Two ways of arguing

| | Rights-based | Consequence-based |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Liberty matters because | People have rights that may not be violated, whatever the benefits | Free societies are richer, more inventive and more peaceful |
| Roots | [[thinkers/locke\|Locke]], [[thinkers/kant\|Kant]] | [[thinkers/smith\|Adam Smith]], [[thinkers/hume\|Hume]], [[thinkers/mill\|Mill]] |
| Leading figures | [[thinkers/nozick\|Nozick]], Murray Rothbard, Ayn Rand | [[thinkers/hayek\|Hayek]], Ludwig von Mises, Milton Friedman |

**The rights-based case.** [[thinkers/nozick|Nozick's]] *Anarchy, State, and Utopia* (1974) is the central philosophical text. Individuals have rights that act as side constraints. A distribution of wealth is just if it arose from just acquisition and voluntary transfer, and redistributive taxation is "on a par with forced labor."

**The consequence-based case.** [[thinkers/hayek|Hayek]] argued that the knowledge needed to run an economy is scattered among millions and can be used only through markets and prices. Milton Friedman's *Capitalism and Freedom* (1962) argued that economic freedom is a condition of political freedom and proposed school vouchers and a negative income tax. Neither wanted a state as small as Nozick's.

**Ayn Rand** (1905–1982), a novelist and philosopher born in Russia, defended capitalism on the basis of an ethic of rational self-interest in *The Fountainhead* and *Atlas Shrugged*. Academic philosophers have mostly ignored her. She has had more readers than all of them together.

## How small a state?

- **Classical liberals** such as Hayek and Friedman accept public goods, a safety net and some regulation.
- **Minarchists** such as Nozick accept only the night-watchman state.
- **Anarcho-capitalists** such as Murray Rothbard and David Friedman would have even police and courts supplied by competing private firms. See [[traditions/anarchism|anarchism]].

## Left-libertarianism

A smaller school, including Hillel Steiner and Michael Otsuka, accepts full self-ownership but denies that anyone can gain unequal private ownership of **natural resources**, which no one made. Land and raw materials belong to everyone equally, so those who take more than their share owe compensation to the rest. The idea goes back to Thomas Paine and the economist Henry George. It leads to conclusions such as a universal basic income funded by a tax on land.

## Objections

- **Original acquisition.** Libertarian property rights depend on a story of how unowned things came to be justly owned. [[thinkers/locke|Locke's]] labour theory has well-known gaps, and actual titles descend from conquest and expropriation. See [[ideas/property|property]].
- **What freedom is.** G. A. Cohen argued that private property restricts freedom as well as protecting it. Every property right is a right to have the state stop others from using a thing. A person without money is unfree to do a great many things.
- **Voluntary or desperate?** An agreement between an employer and someone with no other way to eat is voluntary only in a thin sense. [[traditions/republicanism|Republicans]] call the result domination.
- **Self-ownership proves too much.** It seems to permit selling oneself into slavery, which Nozick accepted and most people do not.
- **Starting points.** Children do not choose their parents' wealth. A system of pure entitlement lets advantage and disadvantage accumulate over generations.
- **Public goods and externalities.** Clean air, public health and a stable climate are poorly supplied by voluntary exchange.

[[thinkers/rawls|Rawls]] rejected libertarianism on the ground that it lets distributive shares be settled by factors "arbitrary from a moral point of view." See [[debates/rawls-vs-nozick|Rawls vs Nozick]].
