# Philosophy and Political Theory Wiki

A plain-language guide to political philosophy, organised around the people who made it. It runs from Plato and Aristotle, through Hobbes and the social contract, to Rawls, Habermas and thinkers still writing. Each thinker has a page on their life, their main ideas, the objections to them and where to start reading.

The questions are old and have not gone away. Why should anyone obey a government? What is justice? How free should people be, and how equal? Who should rule?

## Three thinkers to begin with

**[[thinkers/aristotle|Aristotle]]** (384–322 BC) held that human beings are political animals. We can only live a full life in a community, and the purpose of a city is to help its citizens live well. Politics, on this view, is part of ethics.

**[[thinkers/hobbes|Thomas Hobbes]]** (1588–1679) began the modern tradition by denying that. Political society is not natural. It is a device that frightened, equal individuals build to escape a life that would otherwise be "solitary, poor, nasty, brutish, and short." Authority has to be justified to the people under it.

**[[thinkers/rawls|John Rawls]]** (1921–2002) took Hobbes's device, the social contract, and asked a new question with it. What rules would we choose for our society if none of us knew what place we would have in it?

Most of what lies between and after can be read as argument with one of these three.

## How to use this wiki

1. **The pages summarise. They do not replace the books.** Every thinker page ends with a short "Where to start" list of the chapters worth reading first. Most of the classic texts are short, and many are free online.
2. **Each page gives the objections too.** A thinker is presented in their own terms first, then with the main criticisms. Including a thinker here is not an endorsement. Several held views that are now rightly rejected, and the pages say so.
3. **This is a selection.** It follows the Western canon as it is usually taught, with its gaps. See [[about|About this wiki]] for what is covered, what is missing and how the facts were checked.
