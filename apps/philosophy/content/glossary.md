# Glossary

Short definitions of the terms used across this wiki, with links to the page that explains each in full.

## A

**Alienation.** In [[thinkers/marx|Marx]], the worker's estrangement from the product of work, from work itself, from human nature and from other people.

**Amour-propre.** [[thinkers/rousseau|Rousseau's]] term for the self-love that depends on the opinion of others. Contrasted with *amour de soi*, the simple instinct of self-preservation.

**Anarchy.** The absence of government. In international relations, the absence of any authority above states. See [[traditions/anarchism|anarchism]] and [[traditions/realism|realism]].

**Aristocracy.** Literally "rule by the best." In [[thinkers/aristotle|Aristotle]], rule by a few for the common good. Later, a hereditary upper class.

**Authority.** The right to command and be obeyed, as distinct from the mere power to compel. See [[ideas/authority-and-obligation|authority and obligation]].

**Autonomy.** Self-rule. In [[thinkers/kant|Kant]], the capacity of a rational being to give the moral law to itself.

## B

**Banality of evil.** [[thinkers/arendt|Arendt's]] phrase for great wrongs done by ordinary people who do not think about what they are doing.

**Base and superstructure.** In [[thinkers/marx|Marx]], the economic structure of a society and the legal, political and cultural forms that rise on it.

**Basic structure.** In [[thinkers/rawls|Rawls]], the main institutions of a society taken together, which is what principles of justice apply to.

**Biopower.** [[thinkers/foucault|Foucault's]] term for power that manages the life, health and reproduction of populations.

**Bourgeoisie.** In [[thinkers/marx|Marx]], the class that owns the means of production under capitalism.

## C

**Capabilities.** What a person is really able to do and be. The measure of well-being proposed by [[thinkers/sen|Sen]] and [[thinkers/nussbaum|Nussbaum]].

**Categorical imperative.** [[thinkers/kant|Kant's]] supreme principle of morality: act only on a maxim you could will as a universal law.

**Charisma.** In [[thinkers/weber|Weber]], authority based on belief in a leader's extraordinary personal gifts.

**Checks and balances.** Giving each branch of government some power over the others. See [[ideas/separation-of-powers|separation of powers]].

**Civic virtue.** The qualities citizens need for a free state to last: public spirit, participation, vigilance. See [[traditions/republicanism|republicanism]].

**Civil disobedience.** Open, non-violent lawbreaking in protest at an injustice. See [[ideas/authority-and-obligation|authority and obligation]].

**Civil society.** The sphere of associations, markets and voluntary groups between the family and the state. See [[thinkers/hegel|Hegel]] and [[thinkers/tocqueville|Tocqueville]].

**Class struggle.** In [[thinkers/marx|Marx]], the conflict between those who own the means of production and those who work for them, taken to be the engine of history.

**Common good.** The good of a community as a whole, as opposed to the private good of individuals or factions.

**Communicative action.** In [[thinkers/habermas|Habermas]], action aimed at reaching understanding through reasons, as opposed to action aimed at success.

**Consent.** Agreement to be governed. The basis of legitimate authority for [[thinkers/locke|Locke]]. See [[ideas/social-contract|the social contract]].

**Consequentialism.** The view that actions are right or wrong according to their results alone. See [[ideas/ethical-theories|ethical theories]].

**Constitution.** The basic rules, written or not, by which a state is governed. In [[thinkers/aristotle|Aristotle]], the whole way of life of a city.

**Constitutional patriotism.** [[thinkers/habermas|Habermas's]] idea of loyalty to the principles of a democratic constitution instead of to an ethnic nation.

## D

**Deliberative democracy.** The view that democratic decisions get their authority from public reasoning among equals, not from voting alone. See [[ideas/democracy|democracy]].

**Deontology.** The view that some acts are right or wrong in themselves, whatever their results. See [[ideas/ethical-theories|ethical theories]].

**Dialectic.** In [[thinkers/plato|Plato]], philosophical argument by question and answer. In [[thinkers/hegel|Hegel]] and [[thinkers/marx|Marx]], development through conflict and its resolution.

**Difference principle.** [[thinkers/rawls|Rawls's]] principle that social and economic inequalities are just only if they benefit the least advantaged.

**Disenchantment.** [[thinkers/weber|Weber's]] term for the retreat of magic and mystery from a world explained by science.

**Distributive justice.** Justice in the sharing out of benefits and burdens. See [[ideas/justice|justice]].

**Domination.** Being subject to another's arbitrary power, whether or not it is used. See [[ideas/liberty|liberty]].

## E

**Entitlement theory.** [[thinkers/nozick|Nozick's]] view that holdings are just if they were justly acquired and justly transferred.

**Ethic of responsibility.** In [[thinkers/weber|Weber]], the outlook of one who answers for the foreseeable results of their actions. Contrasted with the ethic of conviction.

**Eudaimonia.** Greek for flourishing or living well. The highest human good in [[thinkers/aristotle|Aristotle]].

**Exception, state of.** An emergency in which normal law is suspended. For [[thinkers/schmitt|Schmitt]], whoever decides on it is sovereign.

**Exploitation.** In [[thinkers/marx|Marx]], the extraction of surplus value from workers by those who own the means of production.

## F

**Faction.** In [[thinkers/madison|Madison]], a group united by an interest opposed to the rights of others or the common good.

**Federalism.** The division of power between a central government and regional ones.

**Forms.** In [[thinkers/plato|Plato]], the perfect and unchanging realities, such as Justice itself, of which things in the world are imperfect copies.

## G

**Genealogy.** A history of how a value or practice came about, written to unsettle it. Used by [[thinkers/nietzsche|Nietzsche]] and [[thinkers/foucault|Foucault]].

**General will.** In [[thinkers/rousseau|Rousseau]], the will of the citizens as a body, aimed at the common good. Distinct from the will of all, the sum of private wishes.

**Governmentality.** [[thinkers/foucault|Foucault's]] term for the techniques by which conduct is directed, by states and others.

## H

**Harm principle.** [[thinkers/mill|Mill's]] rule that power may be used over a person against their will only to prevent harm to others. See [[debates/harm-principle|the harm principle]].

**Hegemony.** Antonio Gramsci's term for rule by consent, in which a dominant group's outlook becomes common sense. See [[ideas/power|power]].

**Historical materialism.** [[thinkers/marx|Marx's]] theory that the way a society produces its living shapes its politics, law and ideas.

## I

**Ideal theory.** Working out what a perfectly just society would be, before asking how to deal with injustice. Associated with [[thinkers/rawls|Rawls]] and criticised by [[thinkers/sen|Sen]] and the [[traditions/realism|realists]].

**Ideology.** A system of ideas. In [[thinkers/marx|Marx]], ideas that present the interests of a ruling class as the interests of all.

**Impartial spectator.** In [[thinkers/smith|Adam Smith]], the imagined fair observer by whose judgement we test our own conduct.

**Individualism.** [[thinkers/tocqueville|Tocqueville's]] word for the withdrawal of democratic citizens into private life. More broadly, the view that the individual comes before society.

**Invisible hand.** [[thinkers/smith|Adam Smith's]] image for the way self-interested actions can serve the public good without intending it.

**Iron cage.** The usual English rendering of [[thinkers/weber|Weber's]] image for the rationalised order of modern capitalism and bureaucracy.

**Is and ought.** [[thinkers/hume|Hume's]] observation that statements of fact do not by themselves yield statements of duty.

## J

**Just war.** The tradition holding that war is permissible only under conditions such as lawful authority, just cause and right intention. See [[thinkers/augustine|Augustine]] and [[thinkers/aquinas|Aquinas]].

## L

**Legal positivism.** The view that what the law is does not depend on its moral merit. See [[ideas/natural-law|natural law]].

**Legitimacy.** The quality of rightful rule. Used both for what people believe ([[thinkers/weber|Weber]]) and for what is actually justified. See [[ideas/authority-and-obligation|authority and obligation]].

**Leviathan.** The biblical sea monster that [[thinkers/hobbes|Hobbes]] took as the name for the sovereign state.

**Lifeworld.** In [[thinkers/habermas|Habermas]], the shared background of meanings and relationships, as opposed to the systems of market and bureaucracy.

## M

**Master and slave morality.** [[thinkers/nietzsche|Nietzsche's]] contrast between the values of the strong and the values invented by the weak in reaction to them.

**Means of production.** The land, tools, factories and machines used to produce goods.

**Minimal state.** A state limited to protecting people against force, theft and fraud. Also called the night-watchman state. See [[thinkers/nozick|Nozick]].

**Mixed constitution.** A government combining rule by one, by a few and by many. See [[ideas/separation-of-powers|separation of powers]].

## N

**Natural law.** A moral law knowable by reason and binding everywhere. See [[ideas/natural-law|natural law]].

**Natural rights.** Rights people hold by nature, before and apart from any government. See [[ideas/rights|rights]].

**Negative liberty.** Freedom as the absence of interference by others. See [[ideas/liberty|liberty]].

**Neutrality.** The liberal principle that the state should not favour any one view of the good life.

**Non-domination.** The [[traditions/republicanism|republican]] idea of freedom: not being subject to anyone's arbitrary power.

## O

**Oligarchy.** Rule by a few, in [[thinkers/aristotle|Aristotle]] the rich, in their own interest.

**Original position.** [[thinkers/rawls|Rawls's]] imagined situation in which people choose principles of justice without knowing their own place in society.

**Overlapping consensus.** In [[thinkers/rawls|Rawls]], agreement on political principles among citizens who hold different religions and philosophies.

## P

**Panopticon.** [[thinkers/bentham|Bentham's]] design for a prison in which inmates can always be watched. For [[thinkers/foucault|Foucault]], the model of modern discipline.

**Paternalism.** Restricting a person's liberty for their own good.

**Patriarchy.** A system in which men hold power over women. See [[traditions/feminism|feminism]].

**Phronesis.** Greek for practical wisdom, the ability to judge what to do in particular cases. See [[thinkers/aristotle|Aristotle]].

**Pluralism.** In [[thinkers/berlin|Berlin]], the view that real values are many and conflict. In political science, the view that power is spread among many groups.

**Polis.** The Greek city-state.

**Polity.** In [[thinkers/aristotle|Aristotle]], rule by the many for the common good, a blend of oligarchy and democracy.

**Popular sovereignty.** The doctrine that final authority belongs to the people. See [[ideas/sovereignty|sovereignty]].

**Positive liberty.** Freedom as self-mastery or self-government. See [[ideas/liberty|liberty]].

**Primary goods.** In [[thinkers/rawls|Rawls]], things every rational person is presumed to want: rights, liberties, opportunities, income and the social bases of self-respect.

**Proletariat.** In [[thinkers/marx|Marx]], the class of wage workers who own no means of production.

**Public reason.** In [[thinkers/rawls|Rawls]], reasons that all citizens could accept, as opposed to those that depend on one religion or philosophy.

**Public sphere.** The space in which private people discuss public matters. See [[thinkers/habermas|Habermas]].

## R

**Rationalisation.** [[thinkers/weber|Weber's]] term for the spread of calculation, rules and expertise through modern life.

**Recognition.** Being acknowledged by others as a free and equal person. A theme from [[thinkers/hegel|Hegel]] taken up by [[thinkers/fanon|Fanon]] and Charles Taylor.

**Reflective equilibrium.** [[thinkers/rawls|Rawls's]] method of adjusting principles and judgements about cases until they fit together.

**Republic.** A state that belongs to its citizens and is governed by law, not by a master. See [[traditions/republicanism|republicanism]].

**Ressentiment.** [[thinkers/nietzsche|Nietzsche's]] word for the suppressed resentment of the weak, which he saw at the root of conventional morality.

**Rule of law.** Government by known, general rules that bind rulers as well as ruled.

## S

**Self-ownership.** The principle that each person owns their own body and labour. See [[traditions/libertarianism|libertarianism]].

**Sittlichkeit.** [[thinkers/hegel|Hegel's]] term, usually translated "ethical life," for the customs and institutions in which freedom is made real.

**Social contract.** The idea that legitimate authority rests on the agreement of the governed. See [[ideas/social-contract|the social contract]].

**Sovereignty.** Supreme authority within a territory. See [[ideas/sovereignty|sovereignty]].

**Spontaneous order.** [[thinkers/hayek|Hayek's]] term for order that results from human action but not from human design, such as language or markets.

**State of nature.** The condition of people without government, real or imagined. See [[ideas/state-of-nature|the state of nature]].

**Surplus value.** In [[thinkers/marx|Marx]], the value workers produce beyond what they are paid, and the source of profit.

## T

**Toleration.** Allowing beliefs and practices one disapproves of. See [[ideas/toleration|toleration]].

**Totalitarianism.** A form of rule that seeks to control every part of life through ideology and terror. See [[thinkers/arendt|Arendt]].

**Tyranny of the majority.** The oppression of minorities or of dissent by a democratic majority. See [[thinkers/tocqueville|Tocqueville]] and [[thinkers/mill|Mill]].

## U

**Utilitarianism.** The view that the right action is the one that produces the greatest total well-being. See [[traditions/utilitarianism|utilitarianism]].

**Utility.** Usefulness. In [[thinkers/bentham|Bentham]], the tendency of something to produce happiness or prevent pain.

## V

**Value pluralism.** See *pluralism*.

**Veil of ignorance.** In [[thinkers/rawls|Rawls]], the device that hides from people in the original position their own class, talents and beliefs.

**Virtù.** In [[thinkers/machiavelli|Machiavelli]], the skill and nerve needed to win and hold power. Not moral virtue.

**Virtue ethics.** The view that ethics is about good character more than rules or results. See [[ideas/ethical-theories|ethical theories]].

## W

**Will to power.** [[thinkers/nietzsche|Nietzsche's]] name for the drive to grow, overcome and master, which he took to be basic to life.
