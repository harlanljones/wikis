# G. W. F. Hegel

Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel was the German philosopher who saw history as the slow working out of human freedom. He argued that freedom is not the absence of ties but something achieved through the right kind of institutions: the family, a market society and a constitutional state.

| At a glance | |
| --- | --- |
| **Lived** | 1770–1831, Germany |
| **Wrote** | *Phenomenology of Spirit* (1807), *Elements of the Philosophy of Right* (1820) |
| **Big idea** | People become free by belonging to a rational social order in which they are recognised by others |
| **Belongs with** | German idealism. A source for [[thinkers/marx\|Marx]], for [[traditions/communitarianism\|communitarians]] and for some [[traditions/conservatism\|conservatives]] |

## Life

Hegel was born in Stuttgart in 1770 and studied theology at Tübingen, where his friends included the poet Hölderlin and the philosopher Schelling. All three welcomed the French Revolution. He finished his first great book, the *Phenomenology of Spirit*, in Jena in 1806 as Napoleon's army took the city. He described seeing the emperor ride out as seeing "the world-soul" on horseback.

After years as a newspaper editor and headmaster, he became a professor at Heidelberg and, from 1818, at Berlin, where he was the most famous philosopher in Germany. He died there in 1831 during a cholera epidemic. His lectures on history, art and religion were published by his students.

He is hard to read. The summaries below simplify.

## The main ideas

### Recognition

A famous section of the *Phenomenology* imagines two self-conscious beings meeting. Each wants the other to acknowledge it as free. They fight, and the one who fears death more submits and becomes the servant of the other. But the outcome is unstable. The master wins recognition only from someone he does not respect, while the servant, by working on the world and shaping it, comes to see his own mind reflected in what he has made.

The lesson is that a person cannot be fully a self alone. Freedom requires **mutual recognition** between equals. This "master and slave" passage influenced [[thinkers/marx|Marx]], [[thinkers/beauvoir|Beauvoir]] and [[thinkers/fanon|Fanon]], and through them the modern politics of recognition.

### History as the progress of freedom

Hegel held that history has a direction. "The history of the world is none other than the progress of the consciousness of freedom." In the ancient East, he claimed, people knew that one was free, the despot. The Greeks and Romans knew that some were free. The modern world, through Christianity and then the Reformation and the French Revolution, knows that all are free.

Ideas develop through conflict. A principle shows its limits, provokes an opposing one, and both are taken up in a fuller view. This pattern is called **dialectic**. (The textbook formula "thesis, antithesis, synthesis" is not Hegel's own.) Philosophy, on his account, understands a form of life only when it is complete: "The owl of Minerva begins its flight only with the onset of dusk."

### Ethical life

The *Philosophy of Right* moves through three stages.

1. **Abstract right** covers persons and property, the world of [[thinkers/locke|Locke]].
2. **Morality** covers the individual conscience, the world of [[thinkers/kant|Kant]]. Hegel thinks Kant's test of universal law is empty by itself and cannot tell us what to do.
3. **Ethical life** (*Sittlichkeit*) is the set of actual institutions and customs in which duties have real content.

Ethical life has three parts of its own.

| Sphere | What holds it together |
| --- | --- |
| The family | Love and natural feeling |
| Civil society | The pursuit of private interest in the market, with law courts and trade associations |
| The state | Conscious commitment to a shared public life |

**Civil society** is one of Hegel's lasting contributions. He took from [[thinkers/smith|Adam Smith]] the idea that self-interest produces a system of mutual dependence. He also saw that it produces great wealth alongside a poor and resentful "rabble," a problem he admitted he could not solve.

The **state** is not a contract made by individuals for their protection. That view, Hegel says, confuses the state with civil society. The state is the community in which people find that their own freedom and the common good are joined. In practice he described a constitutional monarchy with a professional civil service and an assembly of estates.

### The rational and the actual

The preface to the *Philosophy of Right* contains his most disputed line: "What is rational is actual; and what is actual is rational." Critics took it as a blessing on the Prussian state as it stood. Hegel meant something narrower. "Actual" does not mean whatever happens to exist. It means what truly fulfils the idea of freedom, and much that exists falls short of it.

## Criticism and influence

After his death his followers split. The Right Hegelians read him as a defender of church and state. The Left or Young Hegelians, among them the young [[thinkers/marx|Marx]], used his method against both. Marx claimed to have found Hegel standing on his head and turned him right side up, putting material life where Hegel put ideas.

Liberal critics, especially Karl Popper, saw in Hegel's exalted state a source of modern totalitarianism. Scholars since have largely rejected this reading, noting his defence of rights, the rule of law and a sphere of private freedom. His actual blind spots are plain enough: women belong in the family, and the non-European world is written out of history.

His claim that selves are formed by their communities returned in the [[traditions/communitarianism|communitarian]] critique of liberalism, especially in Charles Taylor. Francis Fukuyama's "end of history" is a Hegelian idea.

## Where to start

Do not start with Hegel himself. Read a good introduction first, then the preface and the sections on civil society (182 to 256) in the *Philosophy of Right*, and the introduction to the *Lectures on the Philosophy of History*.
