# Thinkers

One page for each of thirty-five political thinkers, from Plato to Martha Nussbaum. They are listed here by period, in order of birth, with the one idea each is best known for.

Every page has the same shape: a summary, an "at a glance" table, a short life, the main ideas, the criticisms and influence, and where to start reading. At the foot of each page are links to the thinkers born just before and after, so you can read straight through in order. For the same list as a chart, see the [[timeline]].

## The ancient world

Greek and Roman thinkers ask what the good life is and what kind of city makes it possible. The period ends with a Christian answer that looks beyond the city altogether.

| Thinker | Lived | Known for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[thinkers/plato\|Plato]] | c. 428–348 BC | The just city ruled by philosophers. The first great critic of democracy |
| [[thinkers/aristotle\|Aristotle]] | 384–322 BC | Humans as political animals. Virtue, the six constitutions and the middle class |
| [[thinkers/cicero\|Cicero]] | 106–43 BC | The republic as the people's property. Natural law in Latin |
| [[thinkers/augustine\|Augustine]] | 354–430 | The two cities. Government as a remedy for sin |

## Early modern and Enlightenment
