# Carl Schmitt

Carl Schmitt was the German legal theorist who mounted the twentieth century's most influential attack on liberalism, and who joined the Nazi Party in 1933 and put his talents at its service. He argued that politics is at bottom about the distinction between friend and enemy, and that the real sovereign is whoever decides in an emergency.

## Why read him

Schmitt is on this wiki for the same reason he is on university reading lists. His criticisms of liberal democracy are sharp, they have been taken up by thinkers on both the far right and the left, and answering them is a test for any defence of constitutional government. Reading him does not mean endorsing him. His record should be kept in view throughout.

## Life

Schmitt was born in 1888 into a Catholic family in Plettenberg, a small town in Protestant Westphalia. He became a professor of law and, in the 1920s, the best-known constitutional theorist of the Weimar Republic. In its final crisis he advised the governments that ruled by presidential emergency decree.

On 1 May 1933, three months after Hitler came to power, he joined the Nazi Party. He was made a Prussian state councillor and a professor in Berlin. In 1934 he defended the murders of the Night of the Long Knives in an article titled "The Führer Protects the Law." In 1936 he organised a conference on removing "the Jewish spirit" from German law. That same year an SS newspaper attacked him as an opportunist, and he lost his party posts, though he kept his chair until 1945.

After the war he was held by the Allies for over a year and questioned at Nuremberg, but not charged. He refused to go through denazification, never apologised, and was barred from teaching. He lived in Plettenberg until his death in 1985 at ninety-six, receiving a stream of visitors.

## The main ideas

### The exception

*Political Theology* opens with a definition: "Sovereign is he who decides on the exception." Legal rules apply to normal situations. They cannot foresee the extreme emergency or say when it has arrived. Someone must decide that the normal order is suspended and what must be done to restore it, and that someone, not the constitution, holds the real [[ideas/sovereignty|sovereignty]].

Liberal constitutionalism tries to bind all power by law and so to make the sovereign disappear. Schmitt says this is an illusion. The decision can be hidden but not removed. He adds that "all significant concepts of the modern theory of the state are secularised theological concepts." The all-powerful lawgiver is the all-powerful God, and the exception is the miracle.

### Friend and enemy

*The Concept of the Political* looks for what is specific to politics, as good and evil are to morality or beautiful and ugly to art. The answer is the distinction between **friend and enemy**. The enemy is not a private rival or someone morally bad. It is a public adversary, another collective that threatens one's own way of life, and against whom war is a real possibility.

A people exists politically as long as it can make this distinction. Liberalism, Schmitt claims, tries to dissolve politics into economics (competition) and ethics (discussion). It does not abolish enmity. It only disguises it, and wars fought in the name of "humanity" are the cruellest, because the enemy becomes an outlaw of humanity. [[thinkers/hobbes|Hobbes]] is his acknowledged master.

### Against parliamentarism

Parliament, Schmitt argued, rests on the nineteenth-century belief in government by open discussion. In the age of mass parties this is a facade. Real decisions are made in committees and party offices, and the debates persuade no one.

He also drove a wedge between liberalism and [[ideas/democracy|democracy]]. Liberalism is about individual freedom and limiting power. Democracy is about the identity of rulers and ruled, and requires a people that is alike. A dictator acclaimed by the masses could therefore be more "democratic" than a parliament. This argument, built on a one-sided reading of [[thinkers/rousseau|Rousseau]], prepared the ground for what followed.

### The guardian of the constitution

In a debate with the Austrian jurist Hans Kelsen, Schmitt held that the president, not a constitutional court, should be the guardian of the constitution. Kelsen replied that no one should be judge in their own cause, and that the executive is the power a constitution most needs guarding against.

## Criticism and influence

The main reply to Schmitt is that he treats the extreme case as the truth about politics. That a state may have to fight does not show that fighting is what the state is for. His "exception" tends to swallow the rule, and his demand for a people that is alike led, in his own hands, to the exclusion of Jews. His critics see his Nazism not as an accident but as where his thought pointed.

His influence is nonetheless wide. On the left, Chantal Mouffe uses the friend and enemy idea to argue that liberalism hides real conflict, though she tames enemies into "adversaries". Giorgio Agamben built on the state of exception. Debates about emergency powers and executive authority return to him often. He is best read with [[thinkers/weber|Weber]] before him and [[thinkers/arendt|Arendt]] and [[thinkers/habermas|Habermas]] after.

## Where to start

*The Concept of the Political* is short and clear. Then the first chapter of *Political Theology*.
