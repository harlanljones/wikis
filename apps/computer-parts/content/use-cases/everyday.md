# Everyday home, office and school

> **In one sentence:** almost any current computer is fast enough, so spend on the screen, 16GB of memory and an SSD, and skip the graphics card.

This covers web browsing, email, documents and spreadsheets, video calls, streaming, online banking, schoolwork and light photo editing. It is what most computers are used for, and it is the least demanding kind of use.

## What to buy

| Part | Recommendation | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Processor | Any current-generation mid-range chip | Everyday tasks rarely load a modern processor |
| Graphics | Integrated | A graphics card adds cost, heat and noise for no benefit |
| Memory | 16GB | Browsers are the heaviest everyday app. 8GB fills quickly |
| Storage | 256GB to 512GB SSD | An SSD is what makes a computer feel quick |
| Screen | 1080p or better, IPS, 300 nits or brighter | You look at it the whole time |

Good forms for this job:

- **A thin laptop** at $600 to $1,100, if it needs to move.
- **A mini PC** at $350 to $600 plus a monitor, if it does not.
- **A Chromebook** at $300 to $500, if everything you do is in a browser.
- **A refurbished business laptop or desktop** at $150 to $400, for the best value. See [[buying/used-and-refurbished|Buying used and refurbished]].

## Where to spend

1. **The screen.** A bright, sharp, color-accurate screen improves every minute of use. On a desktop, a 27-inch 1440p monitor costs about $160 and is a large step up from a small or old screen. See [[parts/monitor|Monitor]].
2. **Memory.** 16GB keeps the computer responsive with many tabs open and will last years longer than 8GB.
3. **Keyboard, trackpad and webcam.** These decide how pleasant daily use is. Reviews cover them. Spec sheets do not.
4. **Battery life**, on a laptop.
5. **Build quality and warranty.**

## Where not to spend

- **A faster processor.** A Core Ultra 5 or Ryzen 5 handles this workload as well as a Core Ultra 9. You will not notice the difference.
- **A graphics card.**
- **More than 16GB of memory**, unless you keep dozens of heavy tabs and apps open.
- **A faster SSD.**
- **A 4K laptop screen** under 15 inches. It costs battery life for sharpness you can barely see.

## Video calls

Calls are the most demanding everyday task, because they use the processor, camera, microphone and network together.

- **A 1080p webcam** and a decent microphone array. Recent laptops are much better at this than older ones.
- **Good light on your face** improves the picture more than a better camera.
- **A wired connection or strong Wi-Fi.** Choppy calls are nearly always the network. See [[parts/networking|Networking]].
- **16GB of memory**, since a call plus a browser plus office apps will fill 8GB.

## For school

- Check what the school or university requires. Some courses need Windows or specific software.
- Battery life that covers a full day matters more than speed.
- Weight matters if it is carried every day. Under 1.5kg is comfortable.
- For engineering, architecture, film or game design courses, check the software requirements. They may need a dedicated graphics chip. See [[use-cases/creative|Photo, video and 3D work]].
- Use education discounts.

## Working from home

- **Use an external monitor.** Working all day on a laptop screen is hard on the neck.
- **A dock or hub** lets one cable connect the monitor, keyboard, mouse and charger. See [[specs/ports-and-cables#Docks and hubs|Docks and hubs]].
- **Two monitors, or one ultrawide,** help anyone who works across several windows.
- **Check employer requirements** before buying your own machine for work.

## A family computer

A desktop or all-in-one in a shared room works well: a large screen, separate user accounts for each person, and nothing to lose or drop. A mini PC with a 27-inch monitor is the best-value version.

## Keeping it running well

- Keep 15% of the drive free.
- Restart it now and then.
- Install updates.
- Limit what starts automatically.
- Back up. See [[parts/storage#Backups|Backups]].

A computer for everyday use should last six to eight years. If yours feels slow before that, see [[buying/upgrading|Upgrade or replace?]] before buying a new one.
