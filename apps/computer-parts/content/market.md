# The market right now

> **Last updated: 10 November 2026.** Prices are in US dollars and move week to week. Everything else in this wiki is written to stay true for years. This page is the exception, so check its date before relying on it.

## The short version

- **Memory, SSDs and graphics cards cost far more than they did in 2025.** A 32GB DDR5 memory kit that sold for about $80 to $120 in mid-2025 now costs $430 to $550.
- **Graphics cards sell for 1.5 to 2 times their launch prices**, and the fastest ones are hard to find at all.
- **Processors, monitors, cases, power supplies and coolers are priced normally.** Monitors in particular are excellent value.
- **Prebuilt desktops and laptops are often cheaper than building**, because large manufacturers buy memory on long contracts.
- **Analysts do not expect memory prices to fall before 2028.** Waiting for a better deal on memory is not a plan for most people.

## Why prices rose

AI data centers need enormous amounts of memory. The three companies that make almost all of it (Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron) have moved production toward the high-margin memory those data centers buy, and much of their output for the next two years is already sold on long-term contracts. Ordinary PC memory, SSD flash and the memory chips on graphics cards all come from the same factories, so all three got scarce at once.

What this means in practice:

| Part | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| [[parts/memory\|Memory (RAM)]] | Four to five times mid-2025 prices |
| [[parts/storage\|SSDs]] | Roughly two to three times mid-2025 prices |
| [[parts/gpu\|Graphics cards]] | Up 50% to 100% over launch price. Cards with 16GB or more of VRAM are hit hardest |
| Laptops and prebuilt PCs | Up, but by less. Some base models ship with less memory or storage than you would expect at the price |
| Processors, motherboards, monitors, cases, power supplies | Little direct effect. AMD is reported to be planning price rises of around 10% |

DDR4 has risen too, but anyone upgrading an older DDR4 computer can reuse the memory they already own, which is now a real saving. Second-hand memory holds its value and is generally safe to buy. See [[buying/used-and-refurbished|Buying used and refurbished]].

**Intel, socket LGA 1851 (DDR5):**

| Chip | Cores | Price | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core Ultra 5 250K Plus | 18 (6 fast, 12 efficient) | about $220 | Best all-round budget chip |
| Core Ultra 7 270K Plus | 24 (8 fast, 16 efficient) | $255 to $330 | Content creation on a budget |
| Core Ultra 9 285K | 24 (8 fast, 16 efficient) | about $495 | Heavy multi-core work |

**Older platforms that accept DDR4 memory** are still on sale and worth a look if you already own DDR4: AMD's AM4 chips (Ryzen 7 5700X3D and 5800X3D) and Intel's LGA 1700 chips (Core i5-14400F, Core i7-14700K). They are a sensible way to avoid buying memory at today's prices. They are also the end of the line for their sockets.

**What is coming:**

- **Intel Nova Lake (Core Ultra 400)** is expected around early 2027 and is reported to need a new socket, LGA 1954. If that holds, LGA 1851 boards bought today will not take a newer generation.
- **AMD Zen 6 desktop chips** are expected in 2027 and are reported to stay on socket AM5. This makes AM5 the safer platform for anyone who wants to upgrade the processor later.
- Neither has been formally announced with a date. Treat both as rumor until they are.

## What to do about it

- **If your computer works, keep it.** A new SSD or a clean reinstall is cheaper than anything on this page. See [[buying/upgrading|Upgrade or replace?]].
- **If you need a computer now, buy one.** Forecasts say memory will stay expensive through 2027. Waiting a few months is unlikely to help.
- **Price a prebuilt before you build.** Add up the parts on a site such as PCPartPicker and compare with a prebuilt of the same spec. See [[buying/prebuilt-desktops|How to choose a prebuilt desktop]].
- **Buy 16GB unless you know you need more**, and make sure there is a free slot to add memory later. See [[parts/memory|Memory]].
- **Reuse what you can.** Memory, SSDs, the case and the power supply from an old build are worth more than they used to be.
- **Look at used graphics cards** from the previous generation. See [[buying/used-and-refurbished|Buying used and refurbished]].
- **Black Friday is 27 November 2026.** Expect real discounts on monitors, processors and laptops, and smaller ones on memory and graphics cards. See [[buying/when-to-buy|When to buy]].
