# Building a PC

> **In one sentence:** assembling a desktop takes an afternoon and one screwdriver, and the hard part is choosing compatible parts, which a checklist handles.

Building a PC means buying the parts separately and putting them together. The parts only fit one way, nothing needs soldering, and the whole job is closer to assembling furniture than to electronics.

## Should you build?

| Build if | Buy prebuilt if |
| --- | --- |
| You want to choose every part | You want it to work out of the box |
| You enjoy the idea of doing it | The idea fills you with dread |
| You want to upgrade and repair it yourself | You want one warranty and one phone number |
| You already own some of the parts | You are starting from nothing and prebuilts are cheaper this month |

In ordinary years building is cheaper. In late 2026, with memory and graphics cards expensive, a prebuilt is often cheaper than its parts. Price both. See [[buying/prebuilt-desktops|How to choose a prebuilt desktop]] and [[market|The market right now]].

Even when a prebuilt wins on price, building has lasting advantages: you know exactly what is inside, every part is standard, and you can fix or upgrade it for years.

## The process

1. **Decide what it is for** and set a budget. See [[use-cases|What to buy for what you do]].
2. **Choose the parts.** Start from [[buying/budget-tiers|Builds by budget]].
3. **Check compatibility.** Use the [[building/compatibility|Compatibility checklist]].
4. **Buy**, and keep all the boxes until everything works.
5. **Assemble.** Follow [[building/assembly|Putting it together]].
6. **First boot and setup**, also covered there.

## What you need

**Eight parts:**

1. [[parts/cpu|Processor]]
2. [[parts/cooling|Processor cooler]], unless one is in the box
3. [[parts/motherboard|Motherboard]]
4. [[parts/memory|Memory]]
5. [[parts/storage|Storage]]
6. [[parts/gpu|Graphics card]], unless you are using integrated graphics
7. [[parts/power-supply|Power supply]]
8. [[parts/case|Case]]

**And:**

- A Phillips #2 screwdriver, preferably magnetic.
- A USB stick of 8GB or more, to install the operating system.
- A second computer, or a phone, to look things up and make the installer.
- A monitor, keyboard and mouse.
- A clear table with good light.
- Cable ties, usually included with the case or power supply.

Thermal paste comes with the cooler. An anti-static wrist strap is optional: touching the bare metal of the case before handling parts is enough.

## How long it takes

| Stage | First build | Experienced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choosing parts | Several evenings | An hour |
| Assembly | 3 to 5 hours | 45 minutes |
| Installing Windows and drivers | 1 to 2 hours | 1 hour |

## What can go wrong

Very little that is permanent. The realistic risks, in order:

1. **It does not start the first time.** Usual causes are a cable not pushed in fully or memory not seated. See [[building/assembly#If it does not start|If it does not start]].
2. **A part does not fit.** The checklist prevents this.
3. **A part arrives faulty.** Uncommon. Retailers replace it.
4. **Bent socket pins.** The one real hazard. Lower the processor straight down and never touch the pins.
5. **Static damage.** Rare with ordinary care.

## Good resources

- **PCPartPicker** checks compatibility automatically, compares prices and lets you share a list for feedback.
- **The motherboard manual** shows where everything plugs in. Keep it open while you build.
- **A full build video** from a reputable channel, watched once all the way through before you start.
- **Community forums** will review a parts list if you ask.
