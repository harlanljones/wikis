## A method that works for any purchase

1. **Write down what you will do with it**, including the most demanding thing.
2. **Set a budget**, including the monitor, keyboard, mouse and software if you need them.
3. **Find which one or two parts that work depends on.** Spend there.
4. **Buy "good enough" everywhere else.**
5. **Check one independent review** of the exact model.
6. **Check the return policy.** A screen, a keyboard and fan noise can only be judged in person.

## How much to spend

Rough totals for a complete, sensible purchase in late 2026. Prices for anything with memory or a graphics card are higher than usual. See [[market|The market right now]].

| Need | Laptop | Desktop, without monitor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Web, email, schoolwork | $400 to $700 | $350 to $600 (mini PC) |
| General use that should last five or more years | $800 to $1,200 | $600 to $900 |
| Gaming at 1080p | $1,000 to $1,400 | $1,000 to $1,300 |
| Gaming at 1440p | $1,500 to $2,200 | $1,700 to $2,300 |
| Photo and video editing | $1,300 to $2,500 | $1,500 to $2,800 |
| High-end gaming or workstation | $2,500 and up | $3,000 and up |

Below about $350 for a new Windows laptop, the compromises become severe: slow storage, dim screens and too little memory. A refurbished business laptop, a Chromebook, or Apple's budget MacBook is a better buy at that level.
