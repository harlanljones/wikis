# When to buy

> **In one sentence:** buy when you need the computer, because there is always something better six months away, but avoid buying in the few weeks before a known launch or a major sale.

## The short answer

- **If your current computer stops you doing something, buy now.**
- **If it merely feels old, wait** for a sale or the next launch, and consider an [[buying/upgrading|upgrade]] instead.
- **Never wait for "prices to come down" without a specific reason** to think they will.

For what is happening this season, see [[market|The market right now]].

## The yearly rhythm

Hardware follows a fairly regular calendar.

| When | What happens | What it means for you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| January | CES trade show. Laptop makers announce the year's models. Chip makers announce new mobile chips, and often desktop parts | New laptops reach shops from February to April. Last year's models are discounted |
| Late spring | Computex trade show. Motherboards, cases, coolers, and often new processors or graphics cards | |
| June and July | Apple's developer conference. Back-to-school sales begin. Amazon's Prime Day | Good laptop prices, education discounts |
| September and October | Autumn launches from Intel, AMD, Apple and others | |
| Late November | Black Friday and Cyber Monday | The largest discounts of the year on most categories |
| December | Holiday sales, then clearance | |

Graphics card generations arrive roughly every two years, and desktop processor generations every one to two.

## Sales

Real discounts happen at Black Friday, Prime Day and back-to-school. They are largest on monitors, laptops, SSDs, peripherals and last-generation parts, and smallest on newly released, in-demand parts.

To tell a real discount from a fake one:

- **Check the price history.** PCPartPicker shows it for components, and CamelCamelCamel and Keepa show it for Amazon listings.
- **Ignore the crossed-out price.** Compare with what the item sold for last month.
- **Watch for sale-only model numbers.** Some laptops and TVs are made for sales events with cheaper components.
- **Decide what you want before the sale.** A discount on the wrong computer is not a saving.

## Should you wait for the next generation?

A new generation typically brings 10% to 30% more performance at a similar price, and pushes the old generation's price down. That matters if a launch is weeks away. It does not justify waiting six months.

| Situation | Advice |
| --- | --- |
| A replacement is officially announced with a date within about two months | Wait, then buy either the new part or the discounted old one |
| A replacement is rumored | Ignore it. Rumored dates slip |
| The part you want just launched | Wait a few weeks for reviews and for stock to settle. Launch prices are often inflated |
| Your computer is broken or cannot do your work | Buy now |

The first units of a brand-new product sometimes have problems that are fixed with updates in the first months. Buying a mature product late in its life is often the smoothest experience.

## Late 2026: the unusual part

Normally, waiting makes hardware cheaper. Since late 2025, memory, SSDs and graphics cards have done the opposite.

- **Memory prices are forecast to stay high through 2027.** Some chip makers say supply will be tight for longer. Waiting a few months for cheaper memory is unlikely to work.
- **New desktop processors from Intel and AMD are expected in 2027.** Intel's are reported to need a new motherboard. AMD's are reported to fit today's AM5 boards.
- **A refreshed set of Nvidia cards has been reported as delayed** by the cost of memory chips.
- **Black Friday falls on 27 November 2026.** Expect good prices on monitors, processors, laptops and peripherals, and thin discounts on memory and graphics cards.

What follows from this:

- If you need a computer, buy it, and buy only as much memory and storage as you need now.
- If you are building, choose a platform you can add to later: a free pair of memory slots, a spare M.2 slot, a power supply with headroom.
- If your current machine works, the cheapest option is to keep it another year.

## Timing other things

- **Windows versions.** Windows 10 is out of support. Make sure anything you buy runs Windows 11.
- **Returns.** Buy early enough before a deadline to use the return window for testing. Many retailers extend returns over the holidays.
- **Students.** Education pricing is available all year from most makers and gets better during back-to-school promotions.
- **Tax-free weekends and credit card protections** vary by place and are worth a quick check before a large purchase.

## How long should a computer last?

| Kind | Reasonable life | What ends it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desktop for office use | 8 to 10 years | Loss of operating system support |
| Gaming desktop | 4 to 6 years before a graphics card upgrade, 7 to 10 overall | New games |
| Thin laptop | 5 to 7 years | Battery wear, loss of updates |
| Gaming laptop | 4 to 6 years | Heat, battery, new games |
| Mac | 7 to 8 years | End of macOS updates |
| Chromebook | Until its update expiry date | Fixed at launch, up to 10 years |
| Monitor | 8 to 12 years | Usually you just want a better one |

If a purchase would be the third "last a couple more years" fix in a row, it is time to replace.
