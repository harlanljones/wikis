# Desktop or laptop?

> **In one sentence:** buy a laptop if the computer ever needs to leave the desk, and a desktop if it never does.

## The short answer

| If you... | Buy |
| --- | --- |
| Work or study in more than one place | A laptop |
| Only ever use it at one desk | A desktop, or a mini PC |
| Want the most gaming performance for the money | A desktop |
| Want to upgrade and repair it yourself over many years | A desktop |
| Have very little space | A laptop, a mini PC or an all-in-one |
| Need both power at a desk and something to carry | A desktop plus a cheap laptop, or one good laptop with a monitor and dock |

## How they compare

| | Desktop | Laptop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Performance per dollar | Better, especially for games | Worse |
| Portability | None | The whole point |
| Screen, keyboard, webcam | Bought separately | Included |
| Upgrading | Almost everything can be replaced | Storage sometimes, memory sometimes, nothing else |
| Repair | Swap the failed part | Often a whole-board replacement |
| Lifespan | 6 to 10 years with an upgrade or two | 4 to 7 years |
| Noise under load | Can be near silent | Small fans, often loud |
| Ergonomics | Screen at eye level, any keyboard | Hunched, unless docked |
| Power use | Higher | Much lower |

## What "performance per dollar" means in practice

A $1,300 gaming desktop outperforms a $1,300 gaming laptop, because the laptop's price also pays for a screen, a battery and the engineering to make it thin. Laptop chips also run at lower power, so the same model name delivers less. See [[specs/gpu-model-names#Laptop graphics|Laptop graphics]].

For everyday work the gap does not matter. A modern thin laptop is fast enough that most people will never notice a difference from a desktop.

## The options in between

### Mini PC

A box the size of a paperback with laptop-class parts inside, from about $200 to $1,000. It is quiet, uses little power and hides behind a monitor. It is the best choice for a home office that never moves. Most have no room for a graphics card, though the more powerful ones use integrated graphics good enough for light gaming. Apple's Mac mini is the best known.

### All-in-one

A computer built into the back of a monitor, such as an iMac. Tidy, with one cable. The drawback is that the screen and the computer age together, and neither can be upgraded or reused separately.

### Laptop with a dock

A laptop connected by one cable to a monitor, keyboard and mouse at the desk. You get good ergonomics at home and one machine to carry. It works best with a laptop that has Thunderbolt or USB4. See [[specs/ports-and-cables#Docks and hubs|Docks and hubs]].

### Gaming laptop

Real gaming performance in a portable body, with trade-offs: weight of 2 to 3kg, loud fans, two to five hours of battery, and a large charger. A good choice for someone who moves between places and wants to game in each. A poor choice for someone who will leave it on a desk, where a desktop does the job better for less.

### Handheld gaming PC

Devices such as the Steam Deck are complete PCs with a built-in controller and screen. They are a good second device and can be docked to a monitor and keyboard.

### Tablet with a keyboard

Fine for reading, notes, email and drawing. Limited for work that needs many windows, a file system, or desktop software. Check that the specific apps you need exist for it.

## Questions that decide it

1. **Where will you use it in a typical week?** If the honest answer is one desk, a desktop or mini PC gives more for less.
2. **Do you play demanding games?** If yes, a desktop is strongly favored.
3. **Do you want to fix and upgrade it yourself?** Desktop.
4. **Do you already own a good monitor, keyboard and mouse?** That removes $250 or more from the cost of a desktop.
5. **How long should it last?** A desktop can have its graphics card or storage replaced in year four. A laptop cannot.
6. **Does someone else need to use it in another room?** Laptop.

## Late 2026 note

Because memory and graphics cards are expensive, the usual price advantage of building a desktop has shrunk. Large manufacturers buy parts on long contracts, so laptops and prebuilt desktops have risen less than individual components. If you are deciding between building a desktop and buying a laptop of similar ability, price both before assuming the desktop is cheaper. See [[market|The market right now]].
