# Computer Parts Wiki

A plain-English guide to choosing computer hardware. It explains what each part does, what every number on a spec sheet means, and which of those numbers deserve your money.

It is written for two kinds of reader at once. If you have never opened a computer, every page starts with a short answer you can act on. If you already know your way around a motherboard, keep reading past the short answer for the detail.

## The 60-second version

| If you mostly... | Spend on | Do not overspend on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Browse, email, stream, do schoolwork | A good screen, an SSD, 16GB of memory | A fast processor or any graphics card |
| Play games | The graphics card, then the monitor | A top-tier processor, more than 32GB of memory |
| Edit photos and video | Processor cores, memory, a color-accurate screen | Extreme gaming refresh rates |
| Write code | Memory, a fast SSD, a comfortable keyboard and screen | A graphics card (unless you train models) |
| Run AI models locally | Graphics memory (VRAM) above everything else | Processor speed |

Three rules hold for almost everyone:

- **Balance beats any single great part.** A computer is as fast as its slowest relevant part. See [[basics#Balance and bottlenecks|bottlenecks]].
- **Bigger numbers are not always better.** Many spec-sheet numbers are chosen because they look good in an ad. [[specs/marketing-terms|Marketing terms to ignore]] lists them.
- **Buy for what you do now, with one step of headroom.** Hardware gets cheaper and better over time in normal years, so paying heavily for "future proofing" rarely works out.

## Read this before you shop in late 2026

> **Memory and graphics cards are unusually expensive right now.** AI data centers are buying most of the world's memory chips, and the price of RAM, SSDs and graphics cards has risen sharply since late 2025. This changes some standard advice: prebuilt computers and laptops are often better value than building, and 16GB of memory is a sensible stopping point for many people.
>
> [[market|The market right now]] has current prices, the current product generations, and what analysts expect next. It is the one page in this wiki that goes out of date quickly, and it is dated.

## How this wiki is organised

- **Part pages** open with a one-line summary and a "what to buy" table, then explain each spec, then go deeper for technical readers.
- **Time-sensitive facts** such as prices and current model names live on [[market|The market right now]], so the rest of the wiki stays true for years.
- **Links in a different color** point to pages that have not been written yet.
