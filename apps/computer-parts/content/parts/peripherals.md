# Keyboard, mouse and audio

> **In one sentence:** these are the parts you touch and hear for hours a day, so choose them for comfort and ignore almost every number on the box.

## What they do

Peripherals are the things plugged into the computer: keyboard, mouse, headphones, microphone, webcam, speakers. They do not affect performance. They affect how pleasant the computer is to use, and good ones last through several computers.

Preference matters more here than anywhere else in this wiki. Try before you buy where you can.

## What to buy

| Item | A sensible choice | Price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keyboard for typing | A full-size or tenkeyless keyboard you find comfortable. Mechanical if you like the feel | $30 to $100 |
| Keyboard for gaming | A mechanical tenkeyless or 75% board | $50 to $150 |
| Mouse for general use | A comfortable wireless mouse that fits your hand | $25 to $60 |
| Mouse for gaming | A lightweight mouse, 50 to 80 grams, wired or 2.4GHz wireless | $40 to $120 |
| Sound | Good headphones plus a separate microphone beat most "gaming headsets" at the same price | $60 to $200 |
| Webcam | A 1080p webcam and decent lighting | $40 to $100 |

## Keyboards

### Switch type

**Matters: most of all.**

- **Membrane:** a rubber sheet under the keys. Quiet, cheap, soft feel. Most office and laptop keyboards.
- **Mechanical:** an individual spring-loaded switch under each key. More precise, more durable, repairable, and louder.

Mechanical switches come in three feels:

| Feel | What it is like | Typical color name |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Linear | Smooth all the way down | Red, Black, Yellow |
| Tactile | A small bump when the key registers | Brown |
| Clicky | A bump and an audible click | Blue |

Linear is popular for games, tactile for typing. Clicky switches annoy everyone else in the room.

### Layout

**Matters: for desk space.**

| Layout | Keys | What is missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full size (100%) | about 104 | Nothing |
| Tenkeyless (TKL) | about 87 | Number pad |
| 75% | about 84 | Number pad, with the rest squeezed together |
| 65% | about 68 | Number pad and function row |
| 60% | about 61 | Number pad, function row and arrow keys |

Smaller boards leave more room for the mouse. Keep the number pad if you enter numbers often.

### Other keyboard terms

- **Hot-swappable:** switches pull out and can be replaced without soldering.
- **Keycaps, ABS or PBT:** PBT plastic resists the shine that develops on ABS.
- **N-key rollover:** every key registers even when many are pressed together.
- **Hall effect and rapid trigger:** magnetic switches whose trigger point can be adjusted, and which reset the instant you start lifting. Useful in competitive shooters.
- **ANSI and ISO:** the US and European physical layouts. The Enter key shape differs.

## Mice

### Shape and weight

**Matters: most of all.**

The mouse must fit your hand and grip. No spec replaces holding it. Lighter mice (under 80 grams) are easier to move quickly and less tiring.

### Sensor and DPI

**Matters: not any more.**

DPI is sensitivity: how far the cursor moves per inch of hand movement. Every modern sensor is accurate. Most people use 800 to 1,600 DPI. A mouse advertising 30,000 DPI is advertising a number nobody uses.

### Polling rate

**Matters: slightly.**

How often the mouse reports its position. 1,000Hz is standard and enough. 4,000Hz and 8,000Hz exist, cost battery life and processor time, and are imperceptible to most people.

### Wired or wireless

**Matters: for convenience.**

Modern **2.4GHz wireless** mice, the kind with a small USB receiver, are as responsive as wired ones. **Bluetooth** is fine for office work and slightly laggy for games.

## Audio

- **Headphones and a separate microphone** usually sound better than a gaming headset at the same total price. A headset is more convenient.
- **Open-back headphones** sound more natural and leak sound in both directions. **Closed-back** headphones isolate.
- **"7.1 surround" in a headset** is software processing on two speakers. Windows and games can do this for any headphones.
- **USB microphones** are simple and good enough for calls and streaming. **XLR microphones** need a separate audio interface and are for people who know they want one.
- **A DAC or amplifier** is rarely needed. Modern motherboard audio is clean enough for most headphones. Buy one if you hear hiss or buzz, or own headphones that are hard to drive.
- **Speakers:** a pair of powered bookshelf speakers beats any small "computer speaker" set.

## Webcams

Resolution matters less than light. A 1080p webcam facing a window looks better than a 4K one in a dim room. Built-in laptop webcams have improved to 1080p or better on most recent models. For the best quality, phones and mirrorless cameras can be used as webcams.

## Specs you can mostly ignore

- **Mouse DPI above about 3,200.**
- **Polling rates above 1,000Hz.**
- **"Gaming" as a label.**
- **RGB lighting**, as a performance feature.
- **Virtual surround sound.**
- **"Military grade" or "aircraft grade" anything.**
- **Number of programmable buttons**, unless you use them.

## Going deeper

- **Latency chains.** Click-to-screen delay is the sum of the mouse, the USB polling interval, the game, the graphics pipeline and the monitor. Peripherals are a small share. A high refresh [[parts/monitor|monitor]] cuts more delay than any mouse upgrade.
- **Switch details.** Actuation force (in grams), travel distance and actuation point vary between switches of the same type. Factory-lubricated switches are smoother. Switch testers with a dozen samples cost a few dollars.
- **Mounting and sound.** Gasket-mounted keyboards with foam sound softer and deeper than tray-mounted ones. This is the main thing enthusiasts pay for.
- **Wireless interference.** USB 3.0 ports radiate noise in the 2.4GHz band. Put the mouse receiver on a short extension cable near the mouse, away from USB 3 devices.
- **Ergonomics.** Wrists straight, elbows near 90 degrees, top of the screen at or just below eye level. A vertical mouse or a split keyboard can help existing strain. A monitor arm and a decent chair matter as much as either.
- **Controllers.** Xbox controllers work natively on Windows. PlayStation and Switch controllers work through Steam or a small utility.
- **Docks and hubs.** A laptop used at a desk benefits from a USB-C or Thunderbolt dock so one cable connects everything. Check the laptop's port capabilities first. See [[specs/ports-and-cables|Ports and cables]].
- **Uninterruptible power supplies and surge protectors** are covered under [[parts/power-supply|Power supply]].

## Common mistakes

- Buying on specifications instead of comfort.
- Buying a clicky keyboard for a shared room or for calls.
- Buying a 60% keyboard and discovering you needed arrow keys.
- Paying for 7.1 surround.
- Plugging a wireless mouse receiver into the back of a desktop under a metal desk and blaming the mouse for stutter.
- Spending the whole budget on the tower and leaving nothing for the things you touch.
