# Distillation

Distillation trains a small "student" model to imitate a large "teacher", which is how many of the best small open models got good, including the small DeepSeek-R1 variants that run on home hardware.

## Two kinds

- **Logit distillation.** The student is trained to match the teacher's full probability distribution over the next token, not just the one correct token. The teacher's spread of probabilities ("this could be 'car' or 'vehicle', but not 'banana'") carries far more information per example than a single right answer. It needs both models to share a tokenizer and the teacher's outputs to be available at training time. Google's Gemma 2 and 3 small models and Meta's Llama 3.2 1B and 3B were trained partly this way.
- **Sequence distillation,** or training on generated data. The teacher writes answers, reasoning traces or whole textbooks, and the student is fine-tuned on them as ordinary text. It works across tokenizers and even across companies.

## The R1 distills

Alongside DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeek released six smaller models, from 1.5B to 70B, made by fine-tuning existing Qwen 2.5 and Llama 3 models on about 800,000 reasoning traces generated by R1. The distilled models reasoned much better than the same small models trained with reinforcement learning directly.

A common confusion: "DeepSeek-R1 8B" or "R1 14B" on a local model site is one of these distills, a Qwen or Llama model taught to imitate R1. It is not a smaller version of R1 itself, which is a 671B [[fundamentals/mixture-of-experts|mixture-of-experts]] model.

## Limits

- A student is capped roughly by its own size. It learns the teacher's style and many of its skills, but not all of its knowledge.
- Distilled models can imitate the confident tone of the teacher on questions they cannot actually answer.
- Many commercial terms of service forbid using a provider's outputs to train competing models.

## Distillation and quantization

Both make a model smaller, in different ways: distillation reduces the **number** of parameters, [[quantization|quantization]] reduces the **bits** per parameter. They stack. Quantization-aware methods sometimes use the full-precision model as a teacher for its own quantized copy, as in some [[quantization/other-methods#Quantization-aware training|quantization-aware training]].
