# Eastern Sierra

Drive over any Sierra pass and the world changes in about ten miles. The forest thins, the air dries, and the mountains drop away in a single wall to a sagebrush valley 10,000 feet below the crest. US 395 runs along the foot of that wall for 250 miles. It may be the best road in California for geology, and it has more than its share of hard history.

Two things explain the country. First, the mountains take nearly all the rain and snow out of Pacific storms, so the land beyond is desert. Second, this is the western edge of the Basin and Range, where the crust is being pulled apart. The valleys are sinking, the mountains are rising, and magma is close to the surface.
