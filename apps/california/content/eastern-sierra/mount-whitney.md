# Mount Whitney

> **On your hike list:** Mount Whitney Trail, from Whitney Portal near Lone Pine (22 miles round trip, more than 6,200 feet of climbing)

Mount Whitney is 14,505 feet high, the highest point in the United States outside Alaska. Eighty-five miles to the east, on a clear day, you could see into Death Valley, where [[desert/death-valley|Badwater Basin]] is the lowest point in North America. Few places on Earth pack that much relief into so short a distance.

## The wall

From the west, Whitney is the top of a long, gentle slope and hard to pick out. From the east it is the highest tooth in a wall that rises more than 10,000 feet straight out of the Owens Valley. That wall is a fault. The Sierra block is rising and tilting west, the valley is dropping, and the break between them runs along the foot of the range.

It moved on March 26, 1872. At about 2:30 in the morning one of the strongest earthquakes in California's recorded history struck Lone Pine, the town at the foot of the mountain. Almost every adobe building collapsed, and 27 people died, about a tenth of the town. The ground along the fault jumped as much as 20 feet sideways and several feet up. John Muir, shaken out of bed in Yosemite Valley 100 miles away, ran outside shouting, "A noble earthquake!" and watched a cliff fall down.

The fault scarp still crosses the road just west of town, and the mass grave of the victims is beside the highway.

## Clarence King climbs the wrong mountain

In 1864 a party of the California Geological Survey, working in the southern Sierra, saw a cluster of very high peaks and named the highest for their boss, Josiah Whitney. One of them, a 22-year-old Yale graduate named Clarence King, set out to climb it and was turned back a few hundred feet from the top.

King came back in 1871 and reached a summit in the clouds. He announced that he had climbed Mount Whitney and wrote a thrilling account. Two years later a local man rode a mule to the top of the same peak and noticed, a few miles to the north, a mountain that was clearly higher. King had climbed what is now called Mount Langley.

King, by then one of the most famous scientists in America, rushed west to fix his mistake. He reached the real summit in September 1873 and found that he was too late.

## The fishermen

On August 18, 1873, three men from Lone Pine who had been fishing in the Kern River canyon walked up the gentle western slope to the top: Charles Begole, Albert Johnson and John Lucas. They proposed calling it Fisherman's Peak. The name had support in the Owens Valley, where Whitney was not popular, and a bill to make it official made headway in the state legislature before it was killed.

The Paiute name for the mountain is usually given as Tumanguya, "the very old man."

## A trail for mules and scientists

The people of Lone Pine raised money and built a trail to the summit in 1904. Five years later the Smithsonian Institution put up the stone hut that still stands on top, as a shelter for astronomers who wanted to study Mars through thin, dry air. It is the highest building in the lower 48 states. The hut is not a safe place in a storm. A hiker sheltering in it was killed by lightning in 1990.

Whitney is also the southern end of the John Muir Trail, which runs 211 miles from Yosemite Valley along the crest of the range.

## The Alabama Hills

The jumble of round, tan boulders between Lone Pine and the mountain looks much older than the sharp gray peaks behind it. It is the same granite, about the same age. The difference is weathering. The high peaks were shattered by frost. The Alabama Hills rotted slowly under soil in a wetter age and were then uncovered, leaving rounded cores.

Southern sympathizers prospecting here in the Civil War named the hills for a Confederate warship. Since the 1920s Hollywood has used them as India, Arabia, the Old West and other planets, in several hundred films.

## Look for on the trail

- **The 99 switchbacks** between Trail Camp and Trail Crest, blasted into the wall. Somebody counted.
- **The windows.** Just past Trail Crest the path crosses narrow gaps in the ridge with a 2,000-foot drop on the east side.
- **Sky pilot**, a blue-purple flower that grows only above about 11,000 feet and smells strongly of skunk or musk.
- **The summit plateau.** Flat and tilted to the west, it is a piece of an ancient, gentler landscape lifted two and a half miles into the sky.
- **Your own head.** A large share of the people who start do not make the top, and altitude sickness is the usual reason.
