# Further Reading

Books worth having on the shelf or in the pack, followed by a note on where the facts in this wiki come from.

## Geology

- **John McPhee, *Assembling California*.** The best-written account of how the state was put together, told through road trips with a geologist.
- **Keith Meldahl, *Rough-Hewn Land*.** A geologic journey from California to the Rockies, clear and friendly.
- **David Alt and Donald Hyndman, *Roadside Geology of Northern and Central California*.** Organized by highway, for reading in the passenger seat.
- **Mary Hill, *Geology of the Sierra Nevada*.** A compact classic.

## Native California

- **M. Kat Anderson, *Tending the Wild*.** How California Indians managed the land, and what was lost when they were stopped.
- **Malcolm Margolin, *The Ohlone Way*.** An imagined year in the Bay Area before the Spanish.
- **Benjamin Madley, *An American Genocide*.** The documented history of the killing between 1846 and 1873. Hard reading.
- **William Bauer Jr., *California Through Native Eyes*.** State history as told in Native oral tradition.
- **Theodora Kroeber, *Ishi in Two Worlds*.** The famous account, best read alongside more recent work that questions it.
- ***News from Native California*.** A quarterly magazine written mostly by Native Californians.

## Gold Rush and after

- **J. S. Holliday, *The World Rushed In*.** The rush through one forty-niner's diary and letters.
- **H. W. Brands, *The Age of Gold*.** A fast, broad narrative.
- **Louise Clappe, *The Shirley Letters*.** A doctor's wife writes home from the mining camps in 1851 and 1852. Funny and sharp.
- **William Lewis Manly, *Death Valley in '49*.** The rescuer's own story.
- **Kevin Starr, *California: A History*.** The whole state in one volume by its best-known historian.
- **Erika Lee and Judy Yung, *Angel Island: Immigrant Gateway to America*.**
- **Gordon Chang, *Ghosts of Gold Mountain*.** The Chinese workers who built the railroad over the Sierra.

## The Sierra

- **John Muir, *My First Summer in the Sierra* and *The Mountains of California*.** Still the best company on a long climb.
- **Clarence King, *Mountaineering in the Sierra Nevada*.** Tall tales from the man who climbed the wrong mountain.
- **Daniel James Brown, *The Indifferent Stars Above*.** The Donner Party through the eyes of one young bride.
- **Rebecca Solnit, *Savage Dreams*.** Yosemite's hidden history.
- **Jeanne Wakatsuki Houston and James D. Houston, *Farewell to Manzanar*.** A memoir of growing up in the camp.

## Trees and fire

- **Richard Preston, *The Wild Trees*.** The people who climb and measure the tallest redwoods.
- **Joan Dunning, *From the Redwood Forest*.** The fight over the last private old growth.
- **Stephen Pyne, *California: A Fire Survey*.**
- **Obi Kaufmann, *The California Field Atlas*.** Hand-painted maps of everything.

## Water

- **Marc Reisner, *Cadillac Desert*.** The classic, angry history of water in the West.
- **Norris Hundley Jr., *The Great Thirst*.** The scholarly version.
- **Mark Arax, *The Dreamt Land*.** Water, farming and the Central Valley.
- **John Hart, *Storm over Mono*.** The Mono Lake fight.

## Desert and coast

- **Mary Austin, *The Land of Little Rain*.** The Owens Valley and the Mojave in 1903.
- **Susan Casey, *The Devil's Teeth*.** Sharks at the Farallones.
- **Robinson Jeffers, any selected poems.** For Big Sur.
- **Ed Ricketts and Jack Calvin, *Between Pacific Tides*.** The classic tide pool guide, still in print.

## Field guides

- **Sibley's *Field Guide to Birds of Western North America*.**
- **John Muir Laws, *The Laws Field Guide to the Sierra Nevada*.**

## About the facts in this wiki

These pages were written in November 2026 as background reading for a hike list. They are meant to be enjoyable first, but they try hard to be right.

- Heights, distances, dates and counts come mainly from the National Park Service, California State Parks, the U.S. Geological Survey and the parks' own partner organizations, cross-checked against standard histories.
- Trail distances and climbs at the top of each page are copied from the hike list. Check the managing park's page for closures, permits and conditions before you go.
- Some numbers are honestly uncertain, and the pages say so. Population figures for Native California, the number of people who passed through Angel Island, the age of very old trees and the size of Bodie at its peak all vary from source to source.
- A few good stories are probably not true, and those are flagged too: Roosevelt at Burney Falls, Muir on San Jacinto, the naming of the Joshua tree and the Sleeping Maiden of Tamalpais.
- Things change. Mono Lake's level, the status of ranching and elk at Point Reyes, condor and fox numbers and fire recovery are all moving. Treat anything described as recent as a snapshot.
