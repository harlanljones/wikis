# Cabrillo and Point Loma

> **On your hike list:** Bayside Trail and Old Point Loma Lighthouse, Cabrillo National Monument, San Diego (2.5 miles round trip)

On September 28, 1542, three small ships came around the end of this peninsula into what their commander called "a closed and very good port." It was the first time Europeans had set foot on the west coast of what is now the United States. Fifty years had passed since Columbus, and seventy-eight would pass before the *Mayflower*.

## The voyage

Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo had been a crossbowman in the conquest of the Aztec capital and had grown rich in Guatemala on gold mines and forced Native labor. He built the ships himself, on the Pacific coast of Central America. His orders were to explore north, find a route to China, and look for a rumored passage through the continent. Historians still debate whether he was born in Spain or Portugal. A document found in 2015 points to Spain.

He named the bay San Miguel and stayed six days. The Kumeyaay who met him already knew that men with beards, crossbows and horses were killing people inland, news that had traveled hundreds of miles from the Coronado expedition in the Southwest.

The ships went on, passing the Channel Islands and Point Conception, and probably got as far as the Russian River before storms turned them back. They sailed right past the fog-hidden entrances of Monterey Bay and San Francisco Bay. Wintering on one of the Channel Islands, Cabrillo fell on rocks while going to help his men in a fight, shattered a limb and died of the infection on January 3, 1543. His grave has never been found. His pilot took the ships north again in the spring, perhaps to the Oregon border, and then home.

Spain filed the report and did nothing with it for sixty years. In 1602 Sebastián Vizcaíno sailed the same coast and renamed nearly everything, including this bay, which he called San Diego. It was another 167 years before anyone came to stay.

## The lighthouse that was too high

After California became a state, the government picked the top of Point Loma, 422 feet above the sea, as the obvious place for a lighthouse. It was lit in 1855 and on a clear night could be seen 25 miles out.

The trouble was that the nights were often not clear. Fog and low clouds form below the hilltop, so the light was frequently shining above the weather where no ship could see it. On foggy nights the keeper sometimes fired a shotgun to warn ships off. After 36 years the light was shut down and replaced by a new one at the water's edge, which is still working.

The old lighthouse is restored to the 1880s, when Robert Israel kept it with his wife Maria, who served as assistant keeper, and their sons, who rowed across the bay to school.

## The Bayside Trail

The trail is an old Army road. Point Loma guards the only entrance to San Diego Bay, and it has been a military reservation since 1852. Along the way you pass searchlight shelters, a power plant and bunkers from the coastal defense system built between the two world wars to keep enemy ships out of the harbor. The big guns never fired at an enemy.

The slopes are covered in coastal sage scrub, a low, gray, sweet-smelling plant community. It once covered the coast from Ventura to Baja California. Most of it is now under houses, and the patch on Point Loma survived because the Navy would not let anyone build.

## Rock and tide

Point Loma is made of sandstone and shale laid down in the sea about 75 million years ago, in the time of the dinosaurs. Fossils from these layers include ammonites and, a few miles away, the bones of a duck-billed dinosaur that washed out to sea.

On the ocean side, the monument protects one of the best tide pool areas in Southern California. And in winter, gray whales pass the point on one of the longest migrations of any mammal, about 10,000 miles round trip between the Arctic and the lagoons of Baja California. The first whale-watching lookout in the country was set up here in 1950.

## Look for on the trail

- **The Cabrillo statue** by the visitor center, a gift from Portugal, looking over the bay.
- **Navy ships and submarines** going in and out past Ballast Point, where Cabrillo is thought to have landed.
- **Searchlight bunkers** with steel doors, beside the trail.
- **Lemonade berry and black sage.** Crush a sage leaf and smell it.
- **The Fresnel lens** in the old lighthouse tower.
- **Mexico.** The Coronado Islands and the hills of Tijuana are in plain view.
