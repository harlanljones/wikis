# Sierra Nevada

The Sierra Nevada is one block of the Earth's crust, 400 miles long and about 70 wide, tilted up along its eastern edge like a trapdoor. The west side is a long ramp of foothills and forest. The east side is a wall. John Muir thought "Snowy Range," which is what the Spanish name means, sold it short, and called it the Range of Light.

Three things happened here that matter far beyond California. Granite formed in the roots of ancient volcanoes and was later carved by ice into Yosemite. The largest trees on Earth evolved a life that depends on fire. And in 1864, in the middle of the Civil War, the country set aside a valley for everyone, which turned out to be the start of the national park idea.
