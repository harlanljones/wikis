# Redwood National Park

> **On your hike list:** Tall Trees Grove Trail (4.5 miles round trip, 800 foot drop) · Fern Canyon Loop, Prairie Creek Redwoods State Park (1 mile loop)

The tallest known living thing on Earth grows in this park. So do the second, third and most of the rest of the top ten. The park that protects them is partly made of land that had already been clear-cut when the government bought it.

## How Tall Trees Grove was found

In 1963 the National Geographic Society paid for a survey of the remaining redwoods. On a flat beside Redwood Creek, the team measured a tree at 367.8 feet, the tallest then known. The magazine put it in front of millions of readers in 1964, and the "Tall Trees" became the centerpiece of a campaign for a national park.

Timber companies owned the land and kept cutting while Congress argued. The park created in 1968 was a compromise: a thin strip a half mile wide along the creek, nicknamed "the worm," tying together three older state parks. Logging went on right up to the boundary. As at [[north-coast/humboldt-redwoods|Bull Creek]], floods and silt from the bare slopes upstream began to threaten the very grove the park was made for.

In 1978, after loggers drove a convoy of trucks to Washington in protest, Congress added 48,000 acres. Most of it was stumps. The Park Service has spent the decades since removing hundreds of miles of logging roads and thinning the crowded young forest. It is one of the longest-running restoration projects in the country.

## Hyperion

In August 2006 two amateur naturalists, Chris Atkins and Michael Taylor, were bushwhacking through a remote side valley with laser rangefinders when they measured a redwood taller than any on record. A scientist climbed it and dropped a tape from the top: 379 feet. They named it Hyperion. It has grown since and now stands at about 380 feet, taller than a 35-story building.

Hyperion grows on a slope, in a valley that was mostly logged. Clear-cuts stop a few hundred yards away. Its location is not published, and since 2022 the area around it has been closed, because visitors hunting for it were trampling its roots.

The height of a redwood is limited by plumbing. A tree has to pull water from its roots to its top through hollow cells, against gravity, and somewhere around 400 feet the column of water would break. The top leaves of the tallest trees are small and tough like desert plants, even though they sit in one of the wettest forests in the world. They get some of their water straight from fog.

## Fern Canyon

Home Creek has cut a slot through soft bluffs near the beach, and the walls, 30 to 50 feet high, are covered top to bottom in ferns. Most are five-finger ferns, hanging from the seeping rock, with sword ferns, lady ferns and others mixed in. Some of these fern families go back more than 300 million years. Steven Spielberg filmed a dinosaur scene for *The Lost World: Jurassic Park* here.

The bluffs are old river and beach deposits, and they contain flecks of gold. In 1850 prospectors found gold dust in the black sand at their foot, and Gold Bluffs Beach had a brief, disappointing rush. The gold was too fine to recover well.

## Elk and condors

The elk on the prairies and the beach are Roosevelt elk, the largest elk in North America. By the 1920s only a few herds were left in California, and Prairie Creek Redwoods was set aside in 1923 partly to protect them.

This is the homeland of the Yurok, the largest tribe in California today, along with the Tolowa, Chilula and Hupa. In 2022 the Yurok Tribe released California condors near here, the first to fly over the north coast in more than a century. The condor, *prey-go-neesh*, has a central place in Yurok ceremonies for renewing the world.

## Look for on the trail

- **Light at the bottom of the Tall Trees trail.** The grove is on a river flat, where deep flood silt and water grow the tallest trunks.
- **Trees growing on trees.** High in old redwoods, soil builds up on big limbs and supports ferns, huckleberries and even other trees. Salamanders live up there their whole lives.
- **Marbled murrelets.** These seabirds fly inland to lay a single egg on a mossy old-growth branch. Nobody found a nest until 1974.
- **Elk on Gold Bluffs Beach** and in the meadow by the Prairie Creek visitor center.
- **Stumps and second growth** on the drive in. This is what the 1978 expansion bought.
