# Lands End

> **On your hike list:** Lands End Trail, from the Lands End Lookout to Eagle Point (about 3.3 miles round trip)

The trail at Lands End hangs on the cliffs above the Golden Gate, the one gap in six hundred miles of coastal mountains where the sea gets in. Everything that made San Francisco came through this opening, and a fair amount of it sank on the way.

## A river mouth without a river

Twenty thousand years ago you could have walked west from here for more than twenty miles before reaching the surf. So much water was locked up in ice sheets that the shoreline sat out near the Farallon Islands, and the Bay was a dry valley with a big river running through it. That river, the combined Sacramento and San Joaquin, cut the gorge that is now the Golden Gate. When the ice melted, the ocean rose about 400 feet and came in through the gap. San Francisco Bay as we know it is only around 8,000 to 10,000 years old, younger than the villages on its shores.

The gap is also why the Spanish missed the Bay for two centuries. Fog and the offshore angle hid the entrance from passing ships. The first Europeans to see the Bay came on foot, when the lost Portolá expedition climbed Sweeney Ridge in November 1769. No ship sailed in until Juan Manuel de Ayala's *San Carlos* in 1775.

## The graveyard of ships

Strong tides, rocks and fog made the approach deadly. At low tide you can still see pieces of wrecks from the trail: the engine block of the tanker *Lyman Stewart*, which collided with another ship in 1922, and the *Frank H. Buck*, which hit the same rocks in 1937. The freighter *Ohioan* ran aground near Seal Rocks in 1936. The worst wreck of all, the steamer *City of Rio de Janeiro*, went down in fog just inside the Gate in 1901 and took about 128 people with it.

## Adolph Sutro's playground

The level grade you walk on is a railroad bed. In the 1880s a steam train carried city people out along these cliffs to the Cliff House, and when the mining millionaire Adolph Sutro thought the fare was too high, he built his own line and charged a nickel. Sutro had made his fortune by tunneling under the Comstock Lode in Nevada. He spent it here, on gardens open to the public and on the Sutro Baths, which opened in 1896 with seven pools under a vast glass roof, filled by the tides. The baths limped along as an ice rink and burned in 1966. The concrete ruins at the foot of the trail are all that is left.

## Look for on the trail

- **Red, ribbony rock.** The folded layers in the cliffs are radiolarian chert, made from the skeletons of microscopic sea creatures that settled on the deep Pacific floor and were scraped onto the continent.
- **Greenish, slippery rock.** Serpentinite, California's state rock, which came up from the mantle and is the reason these cliffs slide so often. Landslides finished off the railroad in 1925.
- **Mile Rock.** The squat tower offshore was a lighthouse, built in 1906 after the *Rio de Janeiro* disaster and cut down to a helicopter pad in the 1960s.
- **The USS San Francisco Memorial**, built from the shell-torn bridge of a cruiser that fought at Guadalcanal.
