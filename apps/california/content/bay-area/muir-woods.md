# Muir Woods

> **On your hike list:** Muir Woods Main Trail (2 mile loop, flat)

Muir Woods is the reason most people in the world can picture a redwood. It is small, only a few hundred acres of old growth along Redwood Creek, and it survived by a string of lucky accidents and one clever legal move.

## Too steep to bother with

By 1900 almost every redwood within reach of San Francisco Bay had been cut to build, and then rebuild, the cities around it. This canyon was spared because it was awkward. The creek drains to the ocean rather than the Bay, so there was no easy way to float or haul the logs out.

In 1905 William Kent, a wealthy Marin businessman who later served in Congress, and his wife Elizabeth Thacher Kent bought 611 acres of the canyon for $45,000 to keep it from being logged. Kent had to borrow the money. When his wife worried about the debt, he is said to have answered, "If we lost all the money we have and saved these trees, it would be worthwhile, wouldn't it?"

## Saved by a loophole

Two years later a water company moved to condemn the canyon, dam the creek and flood the grove. Kent could not fight a condemnation suit and win, so he went around it. The brand-new Antiquities Act let a president protect land of scientific interest with a signature, and it allowed private owners to donate such land to the government. Kent mailed the deed for 295 acres to Washington. Theodore Roosevelt proclaimed Muir Woods National Monument on January 9, 1908, the first national monument made from donated private land.

Roosevelt wanted to name it for Kent. Kent insisted on John Muir, whom he had not yet met. Muir wrote back that it was "the best tree-lover's monument that could possibly be found in all the forests of the world."

The friendship did not last. A few years later Kent, by then a congressman, voted to dam [[big-picture/water|Hetch Hetchy Valley]] in Yosemite, the defeat that broke Muir's heart.

## A peace conference in the trees

In the spring of 1945, delegates from fifty nations were in San Francisco writing the charter of the United Nations. Franklin Roosevelt had died weeks earlier. On May 19 the delegates came out to Cathedral Grove to hold a memorial for him among the redwoods, on the theory that nothing would better remind them to think in long spans of time. A plaque marks the spot.

## Look for on the trail

- **Family circles.** Redwoods sprout from their own roots, so a ring of trees often marks where a single ancestor stood centuries ago.
- **Burls.** The lumpy growths on trunks are masses of dormant buds, a redwood's insurance policy.
- **Fire scars.** Blackened hollows at the base of big trees record fires from before 1850, many of them set on purpose by Coast Miwok people to keep the land open.
- **Salmon.** Redwood Creek still gets a small winter run of coho, one of the southernmost left.
- **How quiet it is.** Thick, soft bark and a deep carpet of needles soak up sound.
