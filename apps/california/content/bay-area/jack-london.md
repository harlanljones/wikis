# Jack London's Beauty Ranch

> **On your hike list:** Wolf House Ruins and London Lake, Jack London State Historic Park (about 1.2 miles round trip to the ruins)

By 1905 Jack London was the best-paid writer in America. He had been an oyster pirate on San Francisco Bay, a hobo, a sailor, and a failed prospector in the Klondike, and *The Call of the Wild* had made him famous at twenty-seven. That year he bought a worn-out farm on the side of Sonoma Mountain and announced that he was done with cities.

## The ranch

London named it Beauty Ranch and kept buying neighbors' land until he had about 1,400 acres. He threw himself into farming with the same energy he put into everything else. He terraced hillsides to stop erosion, a technique he had seen in Korea and Japan as a war correspondent. He spread manure instead of buying fertilizer, grew spineless cactus on advice from his friend Luther Burbank, and built a circular stone piggery so elaborate that the neighbors called it the Pig Palace. He wrote a thousand words every morning to pay for it all.

## Wolf House

In 1911 London and his wife Charmian began building a house to match. Wolf House had 26 rooms and nine fireplaces, walls of local volcanic stone, unpeeled redwood logs, and a concrete foundation thick enough, London said, to stand for a thousand years. It was designed to survive earthquakes.

On the hot night of August 22, 1913, weeks before they were to move in, it burned. London watched from the hillside. For decades people suspected arson by an angry worker or a jealous neighbor. In 1995 a team of forensic fire investigators studied the ruins and concluded that the likely cause was a pile of linseed-oil-soaked rags, left by workmen finishing the woodwork, which heated up on their own and caught.

London vowed to rebuild and never did. His kidneys were failing, and he died on the sleeping porch of the ranch cottage on November 22, 1916. He was forty. His ashes lie under a rough boulder from the Wolf House site, a short walk from the ruins, next to the graves of two pioneer children.

Charmian later built the House of Happy Walls nearby. It is now the park museum.

## Why the stone is purple

The walls of Wolf House are a clue to the landscape. Sonoma Mountain and the hills around the Valley of the Moon are piled-up lava and ash, the Sonoma Volcanics, erupted between about eight and three million years ago. The same volcanic belt gave wine country its hot springs, its geysers and a good share of its vineyard soils.

## Look for on the trail

- **Fire-reddened stone** and empty window arches at the ruins. The reflecting pool in the courtyard was meant to be stocked with bass.
- **The grave**, marked only by the boulder.
- **The lake and bathhouse** farther up, which London built by damming a creek so his guests could swim.
- **Old-growth redwoods** higher on the mountain, including one called the Ancient Redwood that the loggers skipped.
- **Terraces** still visible on the slopes above the vineyards.
