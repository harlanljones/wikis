# The Oakland Redwoods

> **On your hike list:** Reinhardt Redwood Regional Park Loop, from Canyon Meadow (6.2 mile loop)

The redwoods along the Stream Trail look old. They are about 150 years old, which for a redwood is adolescence. The forest they replaced was one of the most impressive stands of trees on the coast, and it disappeared in about fifteen years.

## The navigation trees

The original forest covered roughly five square miles of the Oakland hills, a pocket of redwoods kept alive by fog pouring through the Golden Gate and piling against the ridge. Some of the trees were giants even by redwood standards. Stumps measured in the 1890s were more than 30 feet across.

Two trees on the ridge stood so tall that they could be seen from outside the Golden Gate, sixteen miles away. Sailing directions published in the 1820s told captains to line up those trees with the tip of Yerba Buena Island in order to miss Blossom Rock, a hazard hidden just under the surface of the Bay. When the trees were cut, the Army Corps of Engineers eventually had to blow up the rock instead.

## Fifteen years of sawing

Logging here started in the 1840s, when sawyers working for the Mexican land grant holders cut timber by hand. The Gold Rush turned it into a frenzy. San Francisco was a city of canvas and wood that burned down six times in two years, and each time it needed lumber again. By the early 1850s there were about ten steam sawmills in these canyons. By 1860 the old growth was gone. Crews later came back for the stumps and even dug out roots for firewood.

The wood built Benicia, Oakland and early San Francisco, including much of what burned in 1906.

## What grew back

Redwoods do not need seeds to return. They sprout from the base of a cut stump, drawing on the root system of the parent. Look for trees standing in circles here: each ring surrounds a spot where one of the giants was felled, and the ring's diameter gives you a feel for the size of the original.

The park opened in 1939 as one of the first in the East Bay Regional Park District, which voters created in 1934, in the middle of the Depression, to buy surplus watershed land. In 2019 it was renamed for Aurelia Reinhardt, the president of Mills College, who sat on the district's first board.

## A fish with a famous address

A plaque along Redwood Creek marks a small claim to fame. In 1855 a naturalist described a trout collected from the San Leandro Creek drainage, which includes this stream, as a new species. Those specimens are tied to the first scientific naming of the rainbow trout, now the most widely transplanted fish in the world. Wild rainbows still migrate up Redwood Creek from the reservoir downstream to spawn in winter. That is why dogs and people are asked to stay out of the water.

## Look for on the trail

- **Stump circles** along the Stream Trail and the French Trail.
- **Ladybugs.** In late fall and winter, thousands of convergent lady beetles cluster on plants and fence posts near the Stream and Prince trails to wait out the cold.
- **The fog line.** Redwoods crowd the canyon bottoms and the foggy west-facing slopes, then stop.
- **Old mill sites**, marked on park maps, with nothing left but flat ground.
