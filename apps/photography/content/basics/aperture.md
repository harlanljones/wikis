# Aperture

The aperture is the adjustable opening inside the lens. A wider opening lets in more light and blurs the background more. A narrower one lets in less light and keeps more of the scene sharp.

## Reading f-numbers

Aperture is written as an **f-number**: f/1.8, f/4, f/11. The f-number is the focal length divided by the diameter of the opening, so a 50mm lens at f/2 has a 25mm opening.

That is why the scale feels backwards: **a smaller f-number is a bigger opening.**

## What aperture changes

**Light.** Opening up one stop doubles the light, which lets you halve the [[basics/shutter-speed|shutter speed]] or the [[basics/iso|ISO]].

**Depth of field.** Wide apertures give a thin slice of sharpness and a soft background. Narrow apertures keep near and far sharp. See [[basics/depth-of-field|depth of field]].

**Sharpness.** Most lenses are a little soft wide open, sharpest two or three stops down, then soften again at very narrow apertures because of **diffraction**, where light bends around the edge of the opening. As a rough guide, diffraction starts to cost visible detail past f/11 on full frame, f/8 on APS-C and f/5.6 on Micro Four Thirds. Higher-resolution sensors show it sooner.

**Sunstars.** Small apertures such as f/11 to f/16 turn points of light into stars. The number of points depends on the number of aperture blades.

## Maximum aperture and "fast" lenses

Every lens has a **maximum aperture**, printed in its name: a 50mm f/1.8 can open to f/1.8. Lenses with a wide maximum aperture are called **fast** because they allow faster shutter speeds.

Zoom lenses come in two kinds:

- **Constant aperture**, such as 24-70mm f/2.8. The maximum aperture is the same at every focal length. These are larger and cost more.
- **Variable aperture**, such as 18-55mm f/3.5-5.6. The maximum aperture shrinks as you zoom in. These are smaller and cheaper.

A fast lens costs, weighs and measures more, and the jump is steep. A 50mm f/1.8 is often around $200 and under 200 g. A 50mm f/1.2 is often over $2,000 and close to a kilogram.

## f-stops and sensor size

An f-number gives the same exposure on any sensor size. f/2.8 is f/2.8 for brightness whether the camera is a phone or medium format. What changes with sensor size is depth of field and total light gathered. See [[cameras/sensor-sizes#Equivalence|equivalence]].
