# Exposure basics

A photograph is a measured amount of light. Three settings decide how much light is used and what the picture looks like as a result. Everything else on a camera is built around them.

## The three settings

| Setting | What it physically is | Unit | Side effect |
| ------- | --------------------- | ---- | ----------- |
| [[basics/aperture\|Aperture]] | The size of the opening in the lens | f-number (f/1.8, f/4, f/11) | [[basics/depth-of-field\|Depth of field]]: how much is in focus |
| [[basics/shutter-speed\|Shutter speed]] | How long the sensor collects light | Seconds (1/1000, 1/60, 2 s) | Motion: frozen or blurred |
| [[basics/iso\|ISO]] | How much the signal is amplified | ISO number (100, 800, 6400) | Noise: grain in the image |

Aperture and shutter speed control how much light reaches the sensor. ISO does not add light. It brightens what was collected, and the noise with it.

## Stops

Photographers count light in **stops**. One stop is a doubling or a halving.

- Shutter speed: 1/250 s lets in twice the light of 1/500 s. That is one stop.
- ISO: ISO 400 is one stop brighter than ISO 200.
- Aperture: f/2.8 lets in twice the light of f/4. Full stops run f/1.4, f/2, f/2.8, f/4, f/5.6, f/8, f/11, f/16. Each step multiplies the number by about 1.4, because light depends on the *area* of the opening.

Stops let you trade one setting for another. These three combinations give the same brightness:

| Aperture | Shutter speed | ISO | What changes |
| -------- | ------------- | --- | ------------ |
| f/8      | 1/125 s       | 400 | Deep focus, moderate motion blur risk |
| f/4      | 1/500 s       | 400 | Shallower focus, action frozen |
| f/4      | 1/125 s       | 100 | Shallower focus, cleanest image |

Most cameras adjust in thirds of a stop, which is why you see values such as f/3.5, 1/160 s and ISO 640 between the full stops.

## How to decide

In practice you pick the setting that matters most for the picture and let the others follow.

1. **Is something moving?** Set the [[basics/shutter-speed|shutter speed]] first.
2. **Does background blur or front-to-back sharpness matter?** Set the [[basics/aperture|aperture]] first.
3. **Let [[basics/iso|ISO]] float** to make the exposure work. A sharp, slightly noisy photo beats a clean, blurry one every time.

The camera's [[basics/exposure-modes-and-metering|exposure modes]] automate exactly this: you choose one setting and the camera fills in the rest.
