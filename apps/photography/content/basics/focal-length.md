# Focal length

Focal length, measured in millimetres, sets how much of the scene a lens takes in. Short focal lengths are wide. Long focal lengths are narrow and make distant things look large.

## The ranges

Focal lengths are usually quoted for a full-frame sensor.

| Full-frame focal length | Name | Diagonal angle of view | Typical use |
| ----------------------- | ---- | ---------------------- | ----------- |
| 14mm to 20mm | Ultra-wide | 114° to 94° | Interiors, big landscapes, night sky |
| 24mm to 35mm | Wide | 84° to 63° | Landscapes, travel, street, environmental portraits |
| 40mm to 60mm | Normal | 57° to 40° | Everyday photos, close to how a scene feels in person |
| 70mm to 135mm | Short telephoto | 34° to 18° | Portraits, details, compressed landscapes |
| 200mm to 400mm | Telephoto | 12° to 6° | Sports, larger wildlife |
| 500mm and up | Super telephoto | 5° and less | Birds, distant wildlife |

A phone's main camera is about 24mm equivalent. Its ultra-wide is about 13mm and its "portrait" lens somewhere from 50mm to 120mm.

## Crop factor

A smaller sensor sees only the middle of the image a lens projects, so the same lens gives a narrower view. The **crop factor** converts a focal length to its full-frame equivalent:

| Sensor | Crop factor | A 50mm lens frames like | To frame like 50mm, use |
| ------ | ----------- | ----------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Medium format (44 x 33 mm) | 0.79 | 40mm | 63mm |
| Full frame | 1.0 | 50mm | 50mm |
| APS-C (Nikon, Sony, Fujifilm) | 1.5 | 75mm | 33mm |
| APS-C (Canon) | 1.6 | 80mm | 31mm |
| Micro Four Thirds | 2.0 | 100mm | 25mm |

The focal length printed on a lens is always its real focal length, including on lenses made only for smaller sensors. An 18-55mm APS-C kit lens frames like a 27-83mm lens on full frame.

See [[cameras/sensor-sizes|sensor sizes]] for the rest of the comparison.

## Perspective and "compression"

Telephoto lenses appear to stack distant objects close together, and wide lenses appear to stretch space. This is caused by **where you stand**, not by the lens. A long lens makes you stand far back, and from far away near and far objects are similar in size. A wide lens lets you stand close, which exaggerates whatever is nearest.

Two practical consequences:

- **Portraits.** Framing a face tightly with a wide lens means standing very close, which enlarges the nose and shrinks the ears. Lenses from 85mm to 135mm put you at a flattering distance.
- **Landscapes.** A wide lens makes distant mountains small. To make a mountain loom over a foreground, back up and use a longer lens.

## Zooming with your feet

Walking closer is not the same as zooming in. Zooming changes only the framing. Walking changes the perspective: the relative size of foreground and background. Choose where to stand for the perspective you want, then choose the focal length to frame it.
