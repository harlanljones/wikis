# Adapters

An adapter lets a lens made for one mount fit a camera with another. Mirrorless cameras are unusually good at this, and it is the reason a move from a DSLR does not have to mean replacing your lenses.

## Why mirrorless cameras adapt so well

Every mount has a **flange distance**: the gap between the mount and the sensor. A lens must sit at exactly its designed distance to focus properly.

DSLR mounts have long flange distances, around 44 to 47 mm, to make room for the mirror. Mirrorless mounts have short ones, 16 to 20 mm. An adapter is essentially a hollow tube that makes up the difference. A lens designed for a longer flange distance can be adapted to a shorter one. The reverse is not possible, so mirrorless lenses cannot go on DSLRs.

| Mount | Flange distance | Type |
| ----- | --------------- | ---- |
| Nikon Z | 16 mm | Mirrorless |
| Fujifilm X | 17.7 mm | Mirrorless |
| Sony E | 18 mm | Mirrorless |
| Micro Four Thirds | 19.25 mm | Mirrorless |
| Canon RF | 20 mm | Mirrorless |
| L-Mount | 20 mm | Mirrorless |
| Leica M | 27.8 mm | Rangefinder |
| Canon EF | 44 mm | DSLR |
| Pentax K | 45.46 mm | DSLR |
| Nikon F | 46.5 mm | DSLR |

Nikon Z has the shortest distance of any current mount, so in principle almost anything can be adapted to it.

## Same-brand adapters

The camera makers sell adapters for their own DSLR lenses. These pass through autofocus, aperture control, stabilization and lens data. Adapted lenses generally work as well as they did on a DSLR, and often better, because focus is measured on the sensor.

| Adapter | From | To | Notes |
| ------- | ---- | -- | ----- |
| Canon EF-EOS R | EF and EF-S | RF | Fully compatible. Versions with a control ring or a drop-in filter slot |
| Nikon FTZ and FTZ II | F | Z | Full function with AF-S, AF-P and AF-I lenses. Older screw-drive AF and AF-D lenses become manual focus |
| Sony LA-EA5 | A | E | For Sony and Minolta A-mount lenses |
| Pentax | K | none | Pentax has no mirrorless mount |

Canon's EF adapter is close to seamless, which is why used EF lenses remain a popular budget route into the RF system.

## Cross-brand adapters

Smart adapters translate between different makers' electronic protocols.

- **Canon EF to Sony E** (Sigma MC-11, Metabones). Works well for stills with many lenses. Continuous autofocus and video are less reliable.
- **Canon EF to L-Mount** (Sigma MC-21). Single autofocus only on most bodies.
- **Sony E to Nikon Z** (Megadap ETZ, Techart). Possible because the Z mount is 2 mm shorter than E. Lets Nikon bodies use Sony-mount lenses with autofocus.
- **Canon EF to Fujifilm X and GFX** (Fringer).

Cross-brand autofocus is always a little less dependable than native, and firmware updates on either side can break it. It is a useful bridge, not a foundation for a system.

## Manual lenses

A plain mechanical adapter with no electronics costs $20 to $100 and fits almost any old manual-focus lens to a mirrorless body: Canon FD, Minolta MD, Olympus OM, Pentax K and M42, Nikon AI, Leica M and many more.

You focus by hand and set the aperture on the lens. The camera's [[cameras/autofocus#Manual focus aids|focus peaking and magnification]] make this easy, and in-body [[cameras/image-stabilization|stabilization]] works once you enter the focal length in the menu.

Vintage lenses cost very little, and many people enjoy their rendering. Expect lower contrast, more flare and softer corners than modern designs.

Leica M lenses adapt to every mirrorless mount, and autofocus adapters exist that move the whole lens back and forth to focus it (Techart, Megadap).

## Speed boosters

A **focal reducer**, sold as a Speedbooster by Metabones and under other names by Viltrox and others, is an adapter with glass in it that shrinks a full-frame lens's image onto a smaller sensor. It widens the view by about 0.71x and gains one stop of light.

A 50mm f/1.8 full-frame lens on an APS-C body with a 0.71x reducer behaves like a 35mm f/1.3. They are popular with Micro Four Thirds and APS-C video shooters.

## Drawbacks

- **Size.** The adapter adds 25 to 30 mm of length for DSLR lenses, cancelling the mirrorless size advantage.
- **Older focus motors** are slower and noisier than modern ones and may not keep up with the fastest burst rates.
- **Teleconverters and some features** may not work through an adapter.
- **Balance.** Heavy DSLR lenses on small mirrorless bodies are front-heavy.

## Should you adapt or replace?

- **Keep and adapt** good DSLR lenses you already own, especially telephotos and specialty lenses that are expensive to replace.
- **Replace** kit zooms and wide-angle lenses first. Mirrorless designs are noticeably better and smaller here.
- **Buy native** for the lenses you use most, particularly for video and fast action.
