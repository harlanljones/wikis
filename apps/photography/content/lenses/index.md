# Lenses

The lens forms the image. It decides the angle of view, how much light reaches the sensor, how the background renders and how much detail there is to record. A good lens on a modest body beats a modest lens on a great body.

Lenses also hold their value and stay useful far longer than cameras. A lens bought today will likely outlast two or three bodies.

## The two numbers

Every lens is described by its focal length and its maximum aperture.

- **[[basics/focal-length|Focal length]]** in millimetres: how wide or narrow the view is. A single number (50mm) is a prime. A range (24-70mm) is a zoom.
- **Maximum [[basics/aperture|aperture]]** as an f-number: how much light it can gather. Smaller numbers are brighter, bigger, and more expensive.

So a **24-70mm f/2.8** is a zoom from wide to short telephoto that can open to f/2.8 at every focal length, and an **18-55mm f/3.5-5.6** is a zoom whose maximum aperture narrows from f/3.5 to f/5.6 as you zoom in.

The rest of the name is a string of abbreviations. See [[lenses/reading-lens-names|reading lens names]].

## Kinds of lens

| Type | Example | What it is for |
| ---- | ------- | -------------- |
| Kit zoom | 18-55mm f/3.5-5.6, 24-70mm f/4 | The general-purpose lens sold with the camera |
| Standard zoom | 24-70mm f/2.8 | The professional workhorse |
| Wide zoom | 16-35mm f/4, 14-24mm f/2.8 | Landscapes, interiors, astrophotography |
| Telephoto zoom | 70-200mm f/2.8, 100-400mm f/4.5-5.6 | Sports, wildlife, portraits, distant landscape details |
| Superzoom | 24-240mm, 18-300mm | One lens for travel, with optical compromises |
| Normal prime | 35mm f/1.8, 50mm f/1.8 | Low light, small size, everyday use |
| Portrait prime | 85mm f/1.8, 135mm f/1.8 | Flattering perspective and strong background blur |
| Macro | 100mm f/2.8 Macro | Life-size close-ups. Also a sharp general lens |
| Super telephoto | 400mm f/2.8, 600mm f/4, 200-800mm | Birds, field sports |
| Pancake | 26mm f/2.8, 28mm f/2.8 | As thin as possible. Makes the camera pocketable |
| Tilt-shift | 24mm f/3.5 TS | Architecture: corrects converging vertical lines |
| Fisheye | 8-15mm | Extreme wide angle with curved lines |

## What makes a lens good

**Sharpness** across the frame, and how it holds up wide open.

**Aberrations.** Chromatic aberration (colour fringes on high-contrast edges), distortion (straight lines bowing), vignetting (dark corners), coma (stars smearing into wings at the frame edge), and flare when shooting toward the sun. Modern cameras correct distortion and vignetting in software, and many compact lenses are designed to depend on that.

**Rendering.** The quality of the out-of-focus blur and the transition into it. Subjective, and the reason people care about particular lenses.

**Autofocus motor.** Linear and voice-coil motors are fast and silent. Stepping motors are quiet and smooth for video. Older designs are slower and noisier.

**Build.** Weather sealing, a metal mount, internal zoom or focus, and useful controls such as an aperture ring or a custom button.

**Minimum focus distance and magnification.** How close it focuses. A lens with 0.5x magnification is a useful near-macro.

## Third-party lenses

Lenses do not have to come from the camera maker. Sigma, Tamron, Viltrox, Samyang (Rokinon), Tokina, Laowa and others make lenses for most mounts, often at 50 to 70 percent of the price of the equivalent own-brand lens and sometimes with no direct equivalent at all.

How welcome they are depends on the mount. Sony E, Fujifilm X, L-Mount and Micro Four Thirds are open. Nikon Z licenses selectively. Canon RF is the most restricted. The [[systems|systems]] pages have the details.
