# Building a lens kit

Most people need two or three lenses. The right ones depend on what you photograph, not on what completes a set.

## Start with what you have

Use the kit lens for a few months before buying anything. Then look at your photos and ask:

- **Which focal lengths do I use?** Most editing software can filter by focal length. If everything is at the wide end, you want a wider lens. If everything is at the long end, you want a longer one.
- **What went wrong?** Too dark and noisy indoors means you need a faster lens. Could not reach the subject means you need a longer one. Could not fit the scene in means wider.

Buying to fix a problem you have actually had is the surest way to spend well.

## Good first additions

**A fast normal prime.** The standard second lens. It transforms indoor and evening photos, and costs little.

**A telephoto zoom.** If you want wildlife, sports or distant details, a 70-300mm or 100-400mm class lens opens up a different kind of photography.

**An upgraded standard zoom.** Replacing a basic kit lens with a 24-105mm f/4, a 17-70mm f/2.8 or a 28-75mm f/2.8 improves everything you already shoot.

## Kits by subject

Focal lengths are full-frame. For APS-C divide by 1.5, and for Micro Four Thirds by 2.

### Travel

- **One-lens kit:** 24-105mm f/4, 24-120mm f/4, or a 24-200mm superzoom
- **Add:** a small fast prime (35mm or 40mm) for evenings and interiors
- Keep it light. The lens left in the hotel takes no pictures.

### Landscape and hiking

- **Wide zoom:** 16-35mm f/4 or 14-30mm f/4
- **Standard zoom:** 24-105mm f/4
- **Optional telephoto:** 70-200mm f/4 or 100-400mm for distant ridgelines and compressed layers
- Choose f/4 over f/2.8. You will be at f/8 to f/11 and will carry it uphill.
- Add a [[lenses/filters|polarizer]] and a light tripod.

For a day hike where weight matters most, a single 24-105mm or a small APS-C body with a 16-80mm class lens covers nearly everything.

### Portraits

- **85mm f/1.8.** The classic. Sharp, affordable and flattering.
- **50mm f/1.8** for half and full body, or small rooms
- **35mm f/1.8** for environmental portraits
- A 70-200mm f/2.8 does all of it for events, at a weight cost.

### Family, kids and pets

- **A fast standard zoom:** 24-70mm f/2.8 or 28-75mm f/2.8
- **A fast prime:** 35mm f/1.8 for indoors
- Fast [[cameras/autofocus|subject-tracking autofocus]] matters as much as the lens.

### Wildlife and birds

- **Starting out:** 100-400mm, or a 150-600mm or 200-800mm class zoom
- **Lighter:** a 300mm f/4 or 400mm f/4.5 or f/6.3 prime, or APS-C or Micro Four Thirds for extra reach
- **Serious:** 180-600mm, 200-600mm, 500mm f/5.6, 600mm f/6.3. Then the exotic 400mm f/2.8 and 600mm f/4 lenses at $12,000 and up
- Birds need at least 400mm equivalent and usually more.

### Sports and action

- **70-200mm f/2.8** for indoor sports and anything within 30 metres
- **100-400mm or 100-500mm** for daylight field sports
- Indoor sport is a low-light problem. f/2.8 or faster is close to mandatory.

### Night sky

- **A fast wide prime:** 14mm f/1.8, 20mm f/1.8, or 24mm f/1.4
- Look for low coma, so stars in the corners stay round
- A sturdy tripod matters more than the camera body

### Macro

- **90mm to 105mm f/2.8 macro.** Reaches 1:1 magnification and doubles as a portrait lens.
- A longer macro gives more working distance from skittish insects.

### Video

- **A standard zoom with constant aperture,** so exposure does not shift when you zoom
- **A wide lens** if you film yourself: 16mm to 20mm equivalent after any stabilization crop
- Look for quiet motors, minimal focus breathing, and power zoom if you zoom during takes

## How much to spend

A common guideline is to spend at least as much on lenses as on the body, over time. A $1,000 body with $1,500 of well-chosen lenses will out-photograph a $2,500 body with a kit lens in almost every situation.

Ways to stretch the budget:

- **Buy lenses used.** They have few wearing parts and lose value slowly. See [[buying/buying-used|buying used]].
- **Choose f/1.8 primes over f/1.4 or f/1.2,** and f/4 zooms over f/2.8.
- **Consider third-party lenses** from Sigma, Tamron and Viltrox.
- **[[lenses/adapters|Adapt]] older DSLR lenses,** which sell for a fraction of their mirrorless equivalents.
- **Rent** specialised lenses for a trip instead of buying.
