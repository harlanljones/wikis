# Glossary

Short definitions of the terms used across this wiki and on camera spec sheets, with links to the page that explains each in full.

## A

**AF-C, AF-S.** Continuous and single autofocus. Canon calls them Servo and One-Shot. See [[cameras/autofocus#Focus modes|focus modes]].

**Anti-aliasing filter.** See *optical low-pass filter*.

**Aperture.** The opening in the lens, written as an f-number. See [[basics/aperture|aperture]].

**Aperture priority (A, Av).** A mode where you set the aperture and the camera sets the shutter speed. See [[basics/exposure-modes-and-metering|exposure modes]].

**APS-C.** A sensor about 23.5 x 15.6 mm, with a 1.5x crop factor (1.6x for Canon). See [[cameras/sensor-sizes|sensor sizes]].

**Aspect ratio.** The shape of the frame. 3:2 for full frame and APS-C, 4:3 for Micro Four Thirds and medium format, 16:9 for video.

## B

**Back-button focus.** Assigning autofocus to a rear button instead of the shutter button.

**Base ISO.** The ISO at which a sensor gives its best quality, usually 100. See [[basics/iso|ISO]].

**Bit depth.** The number of tonal levels recorded per colour channel. 8-bit is 256 levels, 10-bit is 1,024, 14-bit is 16,384.

**Blackout.** The viewfinder going dark between frames in a burst. Stacked sensors avoid it.

**Bokeh.** The visual quality of the out-of-focus areas. See [[basics/depth-of-field|depth of field]].

**Bracketing.** Taking a series of frames while varying one setting, usually exposure or focus.

**BSI.** Backside illuminated sensor. See [[cameras/sensor-technology|sensor technology]].

**Buffer.** Temporary memory that holds burst images before they are written to the card.

**Bulb.** A mode in which the shutter stays open as long as the button is held.

## C

**CFexpress.** A fast memory card format, in Type A and Type B sizes. See [[cameras/memory-cards-and-batteries|memory cards]].

**Chromatic aberration.** Colour fringes along high-contrast edges, caused by a lens focusing colours at slightly different points.

**CIPA.** The Japanese industry body whose test standards define battery life and stabilization ratings.

**Codec.** The method used to compress video, such as H.264, H.265 or ProRes. See [[cameras/video|video features]].

**Coma.** A lens fault that stretches points of light near the frame edge into comet shapes. Matters for night sky photography.

**Contrast detection.** A slower autofocus method that searches for maximum contrast. See [[cameras/autofocus|autofocus]].

**Crop factor.** The ratio of a sensor's size to full frame, used to compare focal lengths. See [[basics/focal-length#Crop factor|crop factor]].

## D

**Depth of field.** The range of distances that appear sharp. See [[basics/depth-of-field|depth of field]].

**Diffraction.** Softening caused by light bending around the edge of a small aperture.

**Distortion.** Straight lines rendered as curves. Barrel distortion bows outward and pincushion bows inward.

**DSLR.** Digital single-lens reflex. A camera with a mirror and an optical viewfinder. See [[cameras/mirrorless-vs-dslr|mirrorless vs DSLR]].

**Dual gain.** Two amplifier circuits per pixel, one for low ISO and one for high. See [[basics/iso#Dual gain|dual gain]].

**DX.** Nikon's name for APS-C.

**Dynamic range.** The span from the darkest to the brightest tones a sensor can record. See [[cameras/dynamic-range-and-noise|dynamic range and noise]].

## E

**EFCS.** Electronic first-curtain shutter. See [[cameras/shutters|shutters]].

**Electronic shutter.** Exposing by reading the sensor, with no moving parts.

**Equivalence.** Comparing lenses across sensor sizes by multiplying focal length and f-number by the crop factor. See [[cameras/sensor-sizes#Equivalence|equivalence]].

**EV.** Exposure value. One EV is one stop.

**EVF.** Electronic viewfinder. See [[cameras/viewfinders-and-screens|viewfinders and screens]].

**Exposure compensation.** A control that makes the camera's automatic exposure brighter or darker.

**Exposure triangle.** The relationship between aperture, shutter speed and ISO. See [[basics|exposure basics]].

## F

**f-number, f-stop.** Focal length divided by aperture diameter. Lower numbers are wider openings.

**Fast lens.** A lens with a wide maximum aperture, such as f/1.4 or f/2.8.

**Flange distance.** The distance from the lens mount to the sensor. See [[lenses/adapters|adapters]].

**Focal length.** The measure, in millimetres, of how wide or narrow a lens's view is. See [[basics/focal-length|focal length]].

**Focus breathing.** A change in framing as a lens focuses. Mostly a concern for video.

**Focus peaking.** A display aid that highlights the sharp edges in the frame.

**Focus stacking.** Combining frames focused at different distances for more depth of field.

**Full frame.** A sensor of 36 x 24 mm, the size of 35mm film.

**FX.** Nikon's name for full frame.

## G

**Global shutter.** A sensor that captures all pixels at the same instant. See [[cameras/sensor-technology#Global shutter|global shutter]].

**Grey market.** Genuine products imported outside official channels, without a local warranty.

## H

**HEIF.** A 10-bit image format that is more efficient than JPEG. See [[basics/raw-vs-jpeg|raw vs JPEG]].

**High-speed sync (HSS).** A flash mode that works above the camera's sync speed by pulsing the light.

**Histogram.** A graph of the brightness values in an image. See [[basics/exposure-modes-and-metering#The histogram|the histogram]].

**Hot shoe.** The mount on top of the camera for a flash or accessory.

**Hybrid camera.** A body designed to be equally capable for stills and video.

**Hyperfocal distance.** The focus distance that gives the greatest depth of field reaching to infinity.

## I

**IBIS.** In-body image stabilization. See [[cameras/image-stabilization|image stabilization]].

**IP53.** A tested rating for dust and water resistance, used by OM System.

**ISO.** The setting that controls image brightness by amplification. See [[basics/iso|ISO]].

**ISO invariance.** A sensor property where brightening a raw file afterward gives similar noise to raising ISO in camera.

## J

**JPEG.** The standard 8-bit processed image format.

## K

**Kelvin.** The unit for colour temperature. See [[basics/white-balance|white balance]].

**Kit lens.** The zoom lens sold in a bundle with a camera.

## L

**Log.** A flat video profile that preserves dynamic range for grading.

**LUT.** Lookup table. A file that converts log footage to a finished look.

## M

**Macro.** Close-up photography. A true macro lens reaches 1:1, or life size on the sensor.

**Mechanical shutter.** A shutter with physical curtains.

**Medium format.** A sensor larger than full frame. See [[systems/medium-format-and-niche|medium format and niche systems]].

**Metering.** How the camera measures light to set exposure.

**Micro Four Thirds.** A 17.3 x 13 mm sensor format and open lens mount. See [[systems/micro-four-thirds|Micro Four Thirds]].

**Mirrorless.** An interchangeable-lens camera with no mirror, using an electronic viewfinder or screen.

**Moiré.** False patterns that appear when fine repeating detail interferes with the sensor's pixel grid.

**Mount.** The interface between body and lens. See [[systems|camera systems and mounts]].

## N

**ND filter.** Neutral density filter. Reduces light evenly. See [[lenses/filters|filters]].

**Noise.** Random variation in brightness and colour, seen as grain.

## O

**OIS, IS, VR, OSS, VC, OS.** Brand names for stabilization built into a lens.

**Open gate.** Video recorded from the sensor's full area instead of a 16:9 crop.

**Optical low-pass filter (OLPF).** A filter that slightly blurs the image to prevent moiré.

**OVF.** Optical viewfinder.

**Oversampling.** Recording more pixels than needed and scaling down for a sharper, cleaner result.

## P

**Partially stacked sensor.** A sensor with extra readout circuitry along its edges, faster than conventional and cheaper than fully stacked.

**Phase detection.** A fast autofocus method that measures the direction and distance to focus.

**Pixel shift.** Combining several frames, with the sensor moved slightly between them, for more resolution.

**Polarizer (CPL).** A filter that cuts reflections and deepens skies.

**Pre-capture.** Buffering frames before the shutter button is fully pressed.

**Prime.** A lens with a single focal length. See [[lenses/primes-and-zooms|primes and zooms]].

## R

**Rangefinder.** A camera focused by aligning two images in a separate optical window. Also used loosely for flat-topped bodies with the viewfinder at the corner.

**Raw.** Unprocessed sensor data saved for editing later. See [[basics/raw-vs-jpeg|raw vs JPEG]].

**Readout speed.** How quickly a sensor can be read from top to bottom.

**Rolling shutter.** Distortion of moving subjects caused by a sensor being read row by row. See [[cameras/shutters#Rolling shutter|rolling shutter]].

## S

**Shutter count.** The number of exposures a mechanical shutter has made.

**Shutter priority (S, Tv).** A mode where you set the shutter speed and the camera sets the aperture.

**Shutter speed.** The length of the exposure. See [[basics/shutter-speed|shutter speed]].

**Stacked sensor.** A sensor with processing circuitry layered behind the pixels for very fast readout. See [[cameras/sensor-technology#Stacked|stacked sensors]].

**Stop.** A doubling or halving of light.

**Subject detection.** Autofocus that recognises people, animals, vehicles and other subjects.

**Sync speed.** The fastest shutter speed at which a normal flash covers the whole frame.

## T

**Teleconverter.** An accessory that multiplies a lens's focal length and reduces its aperture.

**Telephoto.** A lens with a long focal length and a narrow view.

## U

**UHS-I, UHS-II.** SD card bus speeds. UHS-II is faster and has a second row of contacts.

## V

**V30, V60, V90.** SD card ratings for guaranteed minimum write speed, in megabytes per second.

**Vari-angle.** A screen that flips out to the side and rotates.

**Vignetting.** Darkening toward the corners of the frame.

## W

**Weather sealing.** Gaskets and seals that keep out dust and moisture.

**White balance.** The setting that corrects for the colour of the light. See [[basics/white-balance|white balance]].

**Wide angle.** A lens with a short focal length and a broad view.

## X

**X-Trans.** Fujifilm's colour filter layout, used in place of the usual Bayer pattern on most X-series sensors.

## Z

**Zebras.** Stripes overlaid on the display to mark overexposed areas.

**Zoom.** A lens that covers a range of focal lengths.
