# How to read a spec sheet

A camera's specification page is long, and most of its lines do not affect your photographs. This page goes through a typical sheet in order and says what each line means, what a good value looks like, and whether to care.

The worked example is an imaginary mid-range full-frame body.

## Sensor

> 24.5 MP full-frame (35.9 x 23.9 mm) partially stacked BSI CMOS

| Part | Meaning | Care? |
| ---- | ------- | ----- |
| 24.5 MP | Resolution. See [[cameras/megapixels\|megapixels]] | 24 is enough for most uses. 40 and up for heavy cropping and big prints |
| Full-frame | [[cameras/sensor-sizes\|Sensor size]] | Yes. The most important line on the sheet |
| Partially stacked | Readout speed. See [[cameras/sensor-technology\|sensor technology]] | Yes, for action, silent shooting and video |
| BSI | Backside illuminated | Standard now. Slight low-light benefit |

## ISO

> ISO 100-64,000 (expandable to 50-204,800)

The **native range** is what the sensor is designed for. The **expanded** values are digital tricks and best ignored. The top figure says nothing about how usable it is: compare review samples instead. See [[basics/iso|ISO]].

## Processor

> EXPEED 7 / DIGIC X / BIONZ XR / X-Processor 5

The processor generation is a useful shorthand for the autofocus system. Two cameras from the same maker with the same processor usually share subject detection and menus, whatever their price.

## Autofocus

> Hybrid phase-detection AF, 273 points, 96% coverage, detection range -10 to +19 EV
> Subject detection: people, animals, birds, vehicles, aircraft

| Part | Meaning | Care? |
| ---- | ------- | ----- |
| Phase detection | The fast kind. See [[cameras/autofocus\|autofocus]] | Yes. Avoid contrast-only systems for action or video |
| Number of points | How many selectable positions | No, past a hundred or so |
| Coverage | How much of the frame can focus | 90% or more is normal on mirrorless |
| -10 EV | Dimmest light for focusing. Lower is better | A little. Quoted with a very fast lens, so real results are less |
| Subject detection | What the camera can recognise | Yes. Check that your subject is on the list |

## Shutter and burst

> Mechanical shutter 1/8000 to 30 s, electronic to 1/16,000 s. Flash sync 1/200 s
> Up to 14 fps mechanical, 20 fps electronic raw, 60 fps JPEG, pre-release capture

| Part | Meaning | Care? |
| ---- | ------- | ----- |
| 1/8000 s | Fastest mechanical speed | Useful for f/1.4 lenses in bright sun |
| Flash sync | Fastest speed for normal flash | Only if you use flash outdoors |
| fps | Frames per second. See [[cameras/shutters\|shutters]] | 8 to 10 is enough for most action. Check that the figure is "with AF tracking" and in raw |
| Pre-release | Saves frames from before the full press | Very useful for birds and unpredictable action |

Look for the **buffer depth** too: how many raw frames the camera can take before slowing. "200 raw" is generous. "30 raw" is about two seconds at speed.

## Stabilization

> 5-axis in-body VR, up to 8.0 stops

See [[cameras/image-stabilization|image stabilization]]. Having IBIS matters. The difference between a 6-stop and an 8-stop rating matters little in practice.

## Viewfinder and screen

> 0.5-inch 5.76M-dot OLED EVF, 0.8x magnification, 120 fps, 4000 nits
> 3.2-inch 2.1M-dot vari-angle touchscreen

See [[cameras/viewfinders-and-screens|viewfinders and screens]]. Dot count and magnification both matter for the viewfinder. For the screen, the hinge type (tilting or vari-angle) affects daily use more than resolution.

## Video

> 6K 60p N-RAW internal, 4K 60p oversampled from 6K, 4K 120p with 1.5x crop, 10-bit 4:2:2 H.265, N-Log

See [[cameras/video|video features]]. The questions to ask:

- Is 4K **oversampled** or cropped, and at which frame rates?
- Is recording **10-bit** internally?
- Is there a **recording time limit** or known overheating?
- Does it have **microphone and headphone** sockets and a full-size HDMI port?

If you shoot only occasional clips, every current camera is adequate and you can skip this section.

## Storage

> 1x CFexpress Type B / XQD, 1x SD UHS-II

See [[cameras/memory-cards-and-batteries|memory cards and batteries]]. Two slots matter for paid work. Mixed slot types mean buying two kinds of card.

## Battery

> EN-EL15c, approx. 390 shots (CIPA), USB-C charging and power delivery

CIPA figures understate real stills use by half or more. USB-C charging is worth having. Under about 300 shots, plan on a spare.

## Body

> Magnesium alloy, dust and drip resistant, 148 x 103 x 85 mm, 760 g with battery and card

| Part | Meaning | Care? |
| ---- | ------- | ----- |
| Magnesium alloy | Metal chassis. Cheaper bodies use polycarbonate, which is lighter and fine | A little |
| Weather resistant | Seals against rain and dust. Rarely backed by a formal rating, except OM System's IP53 | Yes outdoors. The lens must be sealed too |
| Weight | Check whether it includes the battery | Yes. Add the weight of the lens you will use |

## Connectivity

> Wi-Fi 5 GHz, Bluetooth, USB-C 3.2, full-size HDMI, 3.5 mm mic and headphone

All current cameras transfer images to a phone, with varying grace. USB-C speed matters if you tether to a computer. Full-size HDMI is sturdier than micro HDMI for external monitors.

## What is not on the sheet

The things that most affect how much you like a camera are missing from the specifications:

- How the **grip** fits your hand and where the dials fall
- How quickly you can change the **focus point**
- **Menu** layout and how much you can customise
- How it **balances** with your lenses
- The **look of its JPEGs**
- **Reliability** and how good the maker's repair service is where you live
- The **lenses** available for it, which is the most important thing of all. See [[systems|camera systems and mounts]]

Reviews and a visit to a shop fill these in. Renting for a weekend is better still.

## A quick checklist

1. Sensor size and megapixels suit what I shoot
2. Phase-detection autofocus with subject detection for my subject
3. A viewfinder, unless I am sure I do not want one
4. In-body stabilization, or stabilized lenses
5. A screen hinge that suits me
6. Enough battery life, with USB-C charging
7. Weight I will actually carry, including lenses
8. The lenses I want exist at prices I can pay
