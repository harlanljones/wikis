# Cameras by budget

What each price tier buys in late 2026, with the bodies worth looking at. Prices are approximate US prices for the body only unless noted, and they move. Use them to see where a camera sits, not as quotes.

Remember the rest of the kit. A lens, card and spare battery add $300 to $1,000 or more. See [[buying/first-camera#What to buy with it|what to buy with it]].

## Under $700: buy used

New cameras at this price are compromised. Used ones are not.

| Option | Notes |
| ------ | ----- |
| Used DSLR with kit lens | Canon 80D, Nikon D7200 or D5600. The most camera for the least money |
| Used Sony a6000 or a6100 | Small, light, huge lens choice |
| Used Fujifilm X-T2 or X-T3 | Lovely handling and colour |
| Used Canon EOS RP | Full frame for about $600 |
| New Canon EOS R100 | The cheapest new option. Basic controls and older autofocus |

## Over $5,000: flagships

Built for professional sport, news and wildlife: built-in grips, the fastest sensors, the most durable bodies and the best autofocus each maker has.

| Camera | Stands out for |
| ------ | -------------- |
| Nikon Z9 | 45.7 megapixels, long battery life, now the best-priced flagship |
| Sony a9 III | Global shutter and 120 fps |
| Canon EOS R1 | Sports autofocus and handling |
| Sony a1 II | 50 megapixels at 30 fps in a compact flagship body |
| Fujifilm GFX100 II, Hasselblad X2D II 100C | Medium format |

Few photographers need these. A Z8 or R5 Mark II does nearly everything they do.

## Where the money is best spent

For a fixed total budget, these splits work well:

| Total | Body | Lenses |
| ----- | ---- | ------ |
| $1,000 | Entry-level APS-C with kit lens ($800) | A used 50mm-equivalent f/1.8 ($150 to $200) |
| $2,000 | Nikon Z50 II, Sony a6700 or Fujifilm X-S20 ($900 to $1,400) | Sigma 18-50mm f/2.8 or a kit zoom plus a fast prime |
| $3,000 | Nikon Z5 II or Canon EOS R8 ($1,500 to $1,700) | A 24-105mm or 24-120mm f/4 and a 50mm f/1.8 |
| $5,000 | Nikon Z6 III, Sony a7 V or Canon EOS R6 Mark III ($2,500 to $2,900) | A standard zoom and one specialist lens for your subject |

## Saving money

- **Buy the previous generation.** When a successor launches, the older model is discounted and is still a good camera.
- **Buy used or refurbished.** See [[buying/buying-used|buying used]].
- **Watch for seasonal sales,** which are often largest in late November and around new model launches.
- **Buy the kit.** The lens is much cheaper bundled.
- **Rent before a big purchase,** or for a single trip.
