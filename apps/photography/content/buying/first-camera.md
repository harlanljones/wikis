# Your first camera

This page is for someone buying their first interchangeable-lens camera, probably moving up from a phone. It gives a small number of specific recommendations and explains what to buy alongside the camera.

## Is a camera worth it over a phone?

Phones are excellent in good light at wide and normal focal lengths. A dedicated camera does better at:

- **Telephoto.** Wildlife, sport and distant detail. This is the largest gap.
- **Moving subjects.** Faster, more accurate focus and a real shutter.
- **Low light without the processed look.** A larger sensor gathers far more light.
- **Background blur** that is optical, not simulated.
- **Control and feel.** A viewfinder, dials and a grip change how you take pictures.
- **Editing room.** [[basics/raw-vs-jpeg|Raw files]] hold far more than a phone image.

If you mostly photograph static scenes in daylight and share them on a screen, your phone is already very good. A camera pays off when you want one of the things above.

## What to look for

- **A viewfinder.** It makes the camera usable in bright sun and steadier in the hand. Bodies without one are aimed at video.
- **Current-generation [[cameras/autofocus|autofocus]]** with subject detection. It does much of the work while you learn.
- **Two control dials,** so you can grow into manual control.
- **A [[systems|system]]** with affordable lenses to buy next.

Megapixels, 4K frame rates and burst speed are all more than sufficient on every camera listed here.

### On a tighter budget

Under about $700 for everything, buy used. Good options:

- A used **Sony a6000 or a6100** with kit lens
- A used **Fujifilm X-T2, X-T3 or X-T30**
- A used **Canon EOS RP** for cheap full frame
- A used **DSLR** such as a Nikon D7200 or Canon 80D with a kit lens and a 50mm f/1.8

See [[buying/buying-used|buying used]] and [[systems/dslr-systems|DSLR systems today]].

## What to buy with it

| Item | Notes | About |
| ---- | ----- | ----- |
| Kit lens | Buy the camera with it. Kit zooms are good and cost far less in the bundle | Included |
| Memory card | 64 GB or 128 GB V30 or V60 SD card from a known brand. See [[cameras/memory-cards-and-batteries\|memory cards]] | $15 to $40 |
| Spare battery | One, from the camera maker if you can | $40 to $80 |
| A fast prime | A 50mm equivalent f/1.8. The best value upgrade there is. See [[lenses/building-a-kit\|building a lens kit]] | $150 to $400 |
| A bag or insert | Anything padded. A camera insert in a backpack you already own works well | $20 to $80 |
| Blower and lens cloth | For dust and smudges | $15 |

**Leave for later:** tripod, filters, flash, a second zoom, extra batteries. Buy each when a specific photo calls for it.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Spending everything on the body.** A cheaper body with a second lens is the better kit.
- **Buying the two-lens bundle** with a slow 75-300mm or similar. The second lens in budget bundles is often poor. A fast prime is a better second lens.
- **Buying a full-frame body and then being unable to afford lenses for it.**
- **Buying accessory bundles** padded with cheap filters, tripods and "wide angle adapters". They are low quality.
- **Buying grey market** without understanding that the manufacturer's warranty will not apply.
- **Waiting for the next model.** There is always a next model.

## Your first month

1. Leave the camera in **aperture priority** with **Auto ISO**, and set autofocus to continuous with subject detection on.
2. Learn the **exposure compensation** dial. It is the control you will use most.
3. Read [[basics|exposure basics]] with the camera in your hand.
4. Shoot **raw + JPEG**. Use the JPEGs now and keep the raw files for when you start editing.
5. Take it out often. A hundred ordinary outings teach more than one big trip.
