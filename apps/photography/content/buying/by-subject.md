# Cameras by subject

What you photograph decides which specifications matter. This page takes the common subjects one at a time: what to prioritise, what to ignore, and which cameras and lenses fit.

## Landscape

**Prioritise:** dynamic range at base ISO, resolution if you print large, weather sealing, a good wide lens.

**Ignore:** burst rate, autofocus tracking, video.

Landscapes are forgiving. The subject is still, you shoot at base ISO from a tripod or with stabilization, and any sensor made in the last eight years is excellent. Spend on lenses, a tripod and travel.

| Budget | Body | Lenses |
| ------ | ---- | ------ |
| Lower | Nikon Z5 II, Canon EOS R8, Fujifilm X-T5, used Sony a7R III or Nikon Z7 | A 14-30mm or 16-35mm f/4 and a 24-105mm class zoom |
| Higher | Sony a7R V, Nikon Z8, Canon EOS R5 Mark II | Add a 70-200mm f/4 or 100-400mm |
| Maximum quality | Fujifilm GFX100S II | GF 20-35mm and GF 32-64mm or 45-100mm |

Add a [[lenses/filters|polarizer]] and read about [[basics/depth-of-field#Deep focus for landscapes|deep focus]] and [[cameras/dynamic-range-and-noise#Handling scenes with too much contrast|handling contrast]].

## Hiking and backpacking

**Prioritise:** total weight of body and lenses, weather sealing, battery life, USB-C charging from a power bank.

**Ignore:** anything that adds weight without adding photographs.

Every gram is carried uphill. Weigh the whole kit, not the body. A lighter camera that stays on your shoulder strap gets used far more than a heavier one in the pack.

| Camera | Body weight | A light kit |
| ------ | ----------- | ----------- |
| Fujifilm X-M5 | 355 g | With XC 15-45mm: about 490 g. No viewfinder or weather sealing |
| OM System OM-5 Mark II | 418 g | With 12-45mm f/4 PRO: about 670 g. Sealed to IP53 |
| Fujifilm X-T50 | 438 g | With XF 16-50mm: about 680 g |
| Canon EOS R8 | 461 g | With RF 24-50mm: about 670 g. Full frame |
| Sony a6700 | 493 g | With Sigma 18-50mm f/2.8: about 780 g |
| Sony a7C II | 514 g | With FE 20-70mm f/4: about 1,000 g. Full frame |
| Nikon Z50 II | 550 g | With Z DX 16-50mm: about 685 g |
| Nikon Z5 II | 700 g | With Z 24-120mm f/4: about 1,330 g. Full frame |
| Nikon Z8 | 910 g | With Z 24-120mm f/4: about 1,540 g |

Practical notes:

- **One zoom is usually enough.** A 24-105mm or 24-120mm equivalent covers most trail photography.
- **A chest or strap clip** keeps the camera reachable. Photos do not get taken from inside a pack.
- **Skip the tripod** on long days and lean on stabilization, or carry a small tabletop tripod.
- **Dust, sand and spray** are harder on gear than rain. Sealing matters in deserts and on the coast.
- **Cold shortens battery life.** Keep a spare warm. See [[cameras/memory-cards-and-batteries#Batteries|batteries]].
- **Midday light is harsh.** A polarizer helps, and the hours near sunrise and sunset help more.

## Wildlife and birds

**Prioritise:** autofocus with animal and bird detection, burst rate with tracking, reach, a fast-reading sensor for silent shooting, pre-capture.

**Ignore:** wide-angle lens selection.

The lens matters most and costs most. Budget for it first. Smaller sensors help here: the crop gives extra reach from lighter lenses.

| Budget | Body | Lens |
| ------ | ---- | ---- |
| Starting | Canon EOS R7 or Nikon Z50 II | RF 100-400mm f/5.6-8, or Z DX 50-250mm then the Z 180-600mm |
| Light to carry | OM System OM-1 Mark II | M.Zuiko 100-400mm or 300mm f/4 PRO |
| Mid | Nikon Z6 III, Sony a7 V, Canon EOS R6 Mark III, Fujifilm X-H2S | Z 180-600mm, FE 200-600mm G, RF 200-800mm, XF 150-600mm |
| High | Nikon Z8, Canon EOS R5 Mark II, Sony a1 II or a7R VI | Z 600mm f/6.3 or 800mm f/6.3, RF 100-500mm L, FE 400-800mm G |

Use shutter speeds of 1/1000 s and faster, and get out at dawn when animals are active and the light is good.

## Portraits

**Prioritise:** eye-detection autofocus, a fast prime, pleasing colour. Full frame helps with background blur.

**Ignore:** burst rate, weather sealing.

A fast prime does more for portraits than any body. An 85mm f/1.8 on full frame, or a 56mm f/1.4 on APS-C, is the classic choice.

| Budget | Body | Lens |
| ------ | ---- | ---- |
| Lower | Any current APS-C body | Sigma 56mm f/1.4, Fujifilm XF 56mm f/1.2, Viltrox 56mm or 75mm |
| Mid | Nikon Z5 II, Canon EOS R8 or R6 Mark II, Sony a7 IV | 85mm f/1.8 and 50mm f/1.8 |
| High | Sony a7R V, Canon EOS R5 Mark II, Nikon Z8 | 85mm f/1.2 or f/1.4, 135mm f/1.8, or a 70-200mm f/2.8 |

Light matters more than equipment. A window and a reflector outperform a new lens.

## Sport and action

**Prioritise:** autofocus tracking, burst rate with autofocus, a stacked or partially stacked sensor, buffer depth and fast cards, a telephoto with a wide aperture.

**Ignore:** megapixels beyond 24.

Indoor and evening sport is the hardest test of low-light performance in photography, because you need fast shutter speeds in dim light. That is where f/2.8 lenses and full-frame sensors earn their price.

| Budget | Body | Lens |
| ------ | ---- | ---- |
| Starting | Canon EOS R10 or R7, Nikon Z50 II, Sony a6700 | A 70-300mm class zoom outdoors. A Sigma or Tamron f/2.8 zoom indoors |
| Mid | Nikon Z6 III, Sony a7 V, Canon EOS R6 Mark III | 70-200mm f/2.8, 100-400mm |
| High | Nikon Z8 or Z9, Canon EOS R5 Mark II or R1, Sony a9 III or a1 II | 70-200mm f/2.8, 400mm f/2.8, 100-500mm |

## Travel and everyday

**Prioritise:** size, a versatile zoom, stabilization, USB-C charging, and a camera you enjoy carrying.

**Ignore:** specialist features.

| Style | Body | Lens |
| ----- | ---- | ---- |
| Smallest | Fujifilm X-M5, X-T50 or X-E5, Sony a6700, OM System OM-5 Mark II | A compact zoom and one small prime |
| Small full frame | Sony a7C II, Nikon Z5IIC, Canon EOS R8 Mark II, Panasonic Lumix S9 | 20-70mm f/4, 24-105mm f/4, 28-200mm, or a 40mm prime |
| One lens for everything | Any full-frame body | Nikon Z 24-200mm, Tamron 28-200mm, Canon RF 24-240mm |
| No lens changes at all | Fujifilm X100VI, Ricoh GR IV, Leica Q3 | Fixed. See [[systems/medium-format-and-niche#Fixed-lens cameras with large sensors\|fixed-lens cameras]] |

## Family, children and pets

**Prioritise:** eye and animal detection that works without fuss, a fast lens for indoors, good JPEG colour, easy transfer to a phone.

Children indoors are fast subjects in poor light, which is a harder brief than it sounds. A current body with subject detection and a 35mm or 50mm f/1.8 equivalent prime is the answer. Any camera from the [[buying/first-camera|first camera]] list works, with Canon, Nikon and Sony having the most dependable tracking.

## Night sky

**Prioritise:** a fast, wide lens with low coma, good high-ISO performance, a sturdy tripod. Full frame helps.

| Body | Lens |
| ---- | ---- |
| Any full-frame body. The Nikon Z6 III, Sony a7 IV or a7 V, and Canon EOS R6 series are favourites | 14mm f/1.8, 20mm f/1.8, 24mm f/1.4, or a 14-24mm f/2.8 |
| APS-C on a budget | Sigma 16mm f/1.4, Viltrox 13mm f/1.4, Samyang 12mm f/2 |

Typical settings are the widest aperture, ISO 3200 to 6400, and 10 to 20 seconds. A star tracker mount allows much longer exposures at low ISO.

## Video

**Prioritise:** 10-bit recording, reliable autofocus, no overheating, a microphone input, and the right screen hinge.

| Video first | Canon EOS R6 V, Nikon ZR, Sony FX30 or FX3, Panasonic GH7 |

## Macro

**Prioritise:** a true macro lens, focus bracketing, stabilization, and a screen that tilts for low angles.

Any body works. Buy a 90mm to 105mm macro lens for your mount. Micro Four Thirds has an edge here, with extra depth of field, in-camera focus stacking on OM System bodies, and the 90mm f/3.5 Macro IS PRO that reaches twice life size.
