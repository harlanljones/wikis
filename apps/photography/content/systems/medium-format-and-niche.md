# Medium format and niche systems

Beyond the mainstream systems are cameras built for a narrower purpose: the highest image quality, a particular way of working, or simply the smallest possible package with a large sensor.

## Medium format

"Medium format" in digital cameras means a sensor larger than full frame. The common size is 43.8 x 32.9 mm, about 1.7 times the area of full frame, with a 4:3 shape.

**What it gives you:** very high resolution (100 megapixels is standard), about two thirds of a stop more light gathering, smooth tonal transitions, and files that tolerate heavy editing.

**What it costs:** money, weight, slower autofocus and burst rates, and less depth of field to work with. The advantage over a 45 to 61 megapixel full-frame camera is real but modest, and visible mainly in large prints and demanding commercial work.

| System | Cameras | Character |

Medium format suits landscape, architecture, studio portrait, fashion, product and art reproduction work. It does not suit sport, wildlife or fast events.

## Leica M

The Leica M is a **rangefinder**: you look through a separate optical window and focus manually by aligning two overlapping images. There is no autofocus and no zoom. The mount dates from 1954, and every M lens made since fits the current cameras.

| Camera | Notes |
| ------ | ----- |
| M11 | 60 megapixels, full frame |
| M11-P | Adds content credentials and a discreet finish |
| M11-D | No rear screen at all |
| M11 Monochrom | Black and white only |
| M EV1 | 2025. An electronic viewfinder in place of the rangefinder |

Bodies cost roughly $9,000 and lenses $3,000 to $9,000 each. People buy them for the compact size of the lenses, the direct manual way of working, and the build. M lenses also [[lenses/adapters|adapt]] to every mirrorless mount.

## Fixed-lens cameras with large sensors

These are not interchangeable-lens cameras, but they compete with them. A built-in lens can be designed around the sensor, which allows a much smaller camera.

| Camera | Sensor | Lens | Notes |
| ------ | ------ | ---- | ----- |
| Ricoh GR IV | APS-C, 26 MP | 28mm equivalent f/2.8 | Truly pocketable. The street photographer's camera |
| Fujifilm X100VI | APS-C, 40 MP | 35mm equivalent f/2 | Hybrid viewfinder, IBIS, film simulations |
| Panasonic Lumix L10 | Four Thirds | 24-75mm equivalent f/1.7-2.8 | Released 2026. A fast zoom in a compact body |
| Leica Q3 and Q3 43 | Full frame, 60 MP | 28mm f/1.7 or 43mm f/2 | Stabilized, weather sealed, about $6,000 and up |
| Sony RX1R III | Full frame, 61 MP | 35mm f/2 | High-resolution full frame in a coat-pocket body |
| Fujifilm GFX100RF | Medium format, 102 MP | 28mm equivalent f/4 | Medium format in a body smaller than most full-frame cameras |

A fixed-lens camera makes a good second camera, or an only camera for someone who wants one focal length and no decisions.

## Other niches

**Monochrome cameras.** The Leica M11 Monochrom, Leica Q3 Monochrom and Pentax K-3 Mark III Monochrome have no colour filter. They record sharper, cleaner black-and-white images than a converted colour file, and cannot record colour at all.

**Astrophotography models.** A few bodies have been sold with modified filters that pass the deep red light of hydrogen nebulae, such as Canon's EOS Ra and OM System's OM-3 Astro.

**Infrared and full-spectrum conversions.** Specialist shops remove the filter over the sensor of an ordinary camera so it can record infrared.

**Cinema cameras.** Canon Cinema EOS, Sony FX and Cinema Line, Nikon ZR and RED, Panasonic, and Blackmagic bodies share mounts with the stills systems and prioritise video features. See [[cameras/video|video features]].

**Film.** Film photography has had a revival, and new film cameras have appeared again, including the half-frame Pentax 17 in 2024. That is outside the scope of this wiki.
