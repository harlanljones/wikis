# Fujifilm X and GFX

Fujifilm makes two mirrorless systems and deliberately skips full frame. The **X system** (2012) uses APS-C sensors. The **GFX system** (2017) uses medium format sensors about 70 percent larger than full frame.

## What Fujifilm is known for

- **Design and dials.** Many bodies have marked dials for shutter speed, ISO and exposure compensation, and lenses with aperture rings. You can read the settings with the camera switched off.
- **Film simulations.** Colour profiles modelled on Fujifilm's film stocks: Provia, Velvia, Astia, Classic Chrome, Classic Negative, Acros, Reala Ace and more. Many owners shoot JPEG only because of them.
- **A complete APS-C lens range.** Unlike Canon, Nikon and Sony, Fujifilm designs its best lenses for APS-C, so there are compact f/1.4 and f/2 primes at every focal length and professional zooms sized for the sensor.
- **Size.** A capable kit is noticeably smaller and lighter than full frame.
- **An open mount.** Sigma, Tamron and Viltrox all make X-mount lenses.

## What to watch for

- **No full frame.** You gain portability and give up about a stop of low-light performance and background blur. See [[cameras/sensor-sizes|sensor sizes]].
- **Autofocus tracking** has improved a lot but is generally rated a step behind Canon, Sony and Nikon for fast action.
- **Supply and price.** Popular models, notably the X100VI, have been hard to find, and US prices rose in 2025.
- **Raw processing.** The X-Trans colour filter needs good demosaicing. Current versions of Lightroom, Capture One and DxO handle it well.

The **X100VI** (2024, 40 megapixels, $1,600 at launch) is a fixed-lens compact with a 23mm f/2 lens and a hybrid optical and electronic viewfinder. It is not part of the interchangeable system but is the camera many people picture when they think of Fujifilm.

The **X-T6** has been widely reported as the next release and had not been announced when this page was last checked. See [[whats-new|What changed in 2026]].

## Which one

- **Smallest and cheapest:** X-M5.
- **First camera with a viewfinder:** X-T30 III or X-S20.
- **Travel and everyday:** X-T50 or X-E5 with a small prime.
- **Stills first:** X-T5.
- **Wildlife and sport:** X-H2S.
- **Serious video:** X-H2S or X-H2.

## GFX medium format

GFX uses a 43.8 x 32.9 mm sensor and the G mount. The larger sensor brings very high resolution, smooth tonal gradation and wide dynamic range. Fujifilm has priced these cameras close to high-end full frame, which changed what medium format costs.

| Camera | Released | Megapixels | Notes | Launch price |
| ------ | -------- | ---------- | ----- | ------------ |
| GFX100S II | 2024 | 102 | IBIS, in a body the size of a full-frame DSLR | $5,000 |
| GFX100 II | 2023 | 102 | Flagship. Faster sensor, 8 fps, 8K video, removable viewfinder | $7,500 |
| GFX100RF | 2025 | 102 | Fixed 35mm f/4 lens (28mm equivalent) in a compact rangefinder-style body | $4,900 |

Fujifilm also makes the GFX Eterna 55, a cinema camera built on the same sensor.

GFX suits landscape, studio, architecture and fine art work. It is slower to focus and shoot than full frame, the lenses are large, and it is a poor choice for action. See [[systems/medium-format-and-niche|medium format and niche systems]] for the alternatives.
