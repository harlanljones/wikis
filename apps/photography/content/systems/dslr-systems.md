# DSLR systems today

DSLRs are no longer being developed by Canon or Nikon, but they have not stopped working. Millions are in use, a few are still sold new, and the used market is deep and cheap. This page covers what is left and when one is a sensible purchase.

For how a DSLR differs from a mirrorless camera, see [[cameras/mirrorless-vs-dslr|mirrorless vs DSLR]].

## Where each maker stands

| Brand | Mount | Status |
| ----- | ----- | ------ |
| Canon | EF and EF-S (1987) | No new bodies since 2020. The EOS-1D X Mark III was the last flagship. Lenses are being discontinued a few at a time |
| Nikon | F (1959) | No new bodies since the D6 and D780 in 2020. Development has moved to Z |
| Pentax (Ricoh) | K (1975) | Still committed to DSLRs and has said so publicly |
| Sony | A | Discontinued in 2021 |

The models still available new vary by country and shrink each year. Check that a model is actually in stock before planning around it.

## The case for a used DSLR

A used DSLR and lens is the cheapest way into photography with a large sensor. Prices have fallen as owners move to mirrorless.

| Budget for body and lens | Look for |
| ------------------------ | -------- |
| Under $300 | Canon Rebel T5i, T6i or SL2. Nikon D3300, D3400 or D5300. With the 18-55mm kit lens |
| $300 to $600 | Canon 80D or 77D. Nikon D7200 or D5600. Pentax K-70. Add a 50mm f/1.8 or 35mm f/1.8 |
| $600 to $1,000 | Canon 6D or 5D Mark III. Nikon D750 or D610. Full frame, with a 50mm f/1.8 |
| $1,000 to $1,800 | Nikon D850 or D810. Canon 5D Mark IV. Professional bodies at a fraction of their original price |

Used DSLR lenses are equally inexpensive, and good ones hold their value because they [[lenses/adapters|adapt]] to mirrorless bodies.

## The case against

- **No future bodies.** When the camera fails or you want to upgrade, the path is mirrorless. Your lenses can come with you by adapter on Canon and Nikon.
- **Autofocus.** A current $900 mirrorless body tracks moving subjects better than most DSLRs ever made, across the whole frame, with eye detection. See [[cameras/autofocus|autofocus]].
- **Video** is far behind.
- **Repair support is finite.** Makers typically service a model for about seven to ten years after it is discontinued, as long as parts last.
- **You do not see the exposure** before you shoot, which slows learning.

## Buying advice

- If the budget is under about $600 for everything, a used DSLR is still the strongest option.
- Above that, compare with used mirrorless bodies from 2018 to 2021, such as the Sony a7 III, Nikon Z6, Canon EOS RP or Fujifilm X-T3. They cost a little more and are a better long-term base.
- Choose Canon EF or Nikon F over smaller systems for the adapter path.
- Check the shutter count and condition. See [[buying/buying-used|buying used]].
- Nikon's cheapest DSLRs (D3000 and D5000 series) have no focus motor in the body, so older AF and AF-D lenses will not autofocus on them. AF-S and AF-P lenses are fine.
