# Micro Four Thirds

Micro Four Thirds (MFT or M43) was the first mirrorless system, launched by Panasonic and Olympus in 2008. It is an open standard: any MFT lens fits any MFT body, regardless of brand. The two body makers today are **OM System** (the former Olympus camera division, independent since 2021) and **Panasonic Lumix**.

The sensor measures 17.3 x 13 mm, a quarter of the area of full frame, with a 2x crop factor and a 4:3 shape.

## What Micro Four Thirds is known for

- **Small lenses.** The real advantage. A 600mm-equivalent view comes from a 300mm lens. A weather-sealed 24-200mm equivalent f/4 zoom weighs 560 g. A full kit fits in a small shoulder bag.
- **Stabilization.** A small sensor is easier to move, and these bodies have the most effective IBIS available. Handheld exposures of a second or more are realistic.
- **Weather sealing.** OM System's better bodies carry an IP53 rating, a tested standard that most cameras do not claim.
- **Computational features** on OM System bodies: handheld high-resolution mode, Live ND and Live Graduated ND (simulated filters), Live Composite for star trails and light painting, Pro Capture pre-burst, and in-camera focus stacking.
- **Reach for wildlife.** Long equivalent focal lengths in lenses you can carry all day.
- **Video.** The Panasonic GH line has been a filmmaker's favourite for over a decade.

## What to watch for

- **Low light.** About two stops behind full frame at the same f-number. A fast prime and modern denoising narrow the gap, but it is real.
- **Background blur.** An f/1.8 lens here gives the depth of field of f/3.6 on full frame.
- **Resolution.** Sensors are 20 or 25 megapixels, with less room to crop.
- **Size creep.** The flagship bodies are as large as full-frame cameras. The size saving is in the lenses.
- **Pace of development.** New releases are less frequent than in the full-frame systems.

## Which one

- **Hiking and travel:** OM-5 Mark II with the 12-45mm f/4 PRO or 14-150mm.
- **Birds and wildlife:** OM-1 Mark II with the 100-400mm, or the 300mm f/4 PRO.
- **Style and everyday:** OM-3 with a small prime.
- **Video:** GH7.
- **Hybrid stills and video:** G9 II.

## Who it suits

Micro Four Thirds makes the most sense when the size and weight of the whole kit is the priority: long hikes, travel, birding on foot, and work in rain and dust. It makes the least sense for indoor events and portraits, where larger sensors have a clear advantage.
