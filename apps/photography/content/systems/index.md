# Camera systems and mounts

A camera body only accepts lenses made for its **mount**. Choosing a brand is really choosing a mount, and with it a catalogue of lenses, a set of third-party options, and a used market. Bodies come and go every few years. The mount is the long-term commitment.

There is no bad system. Every one listed here makes excellent cameras, and the differences are in lens selection, price, size and character.

## The systems at a glance

| System | Sensor formats | Launched | Strengths | Watch for |
| ------ | -------------- | -------- | --------- | --------- |
| [[systems/canon\|Canon RF]] | Full frame, APS-C | 2018 | Autofocus, ergonomics, colour, unique lenses, huge EF back catalogue via adapter | The most restricted mount for third-party autofocus lenses |
| [[systems/nikon\|Nikon Z]] | Full frame, APS-C | 2018 | Value for money, excellent S-Line lenses, lightweight telephotos, RED-derived video | Fewer native APS-C lenses |
| [[systems/sony\|Sony E]] | Full frame, APS-C | 2010 | Largest lens selection of any mirrorless mount, compact full-frame bodies | Menus and handling divide opinion |
| [[systems/fujifilm\|Fujifilm X]] | APS-C | 2012 | Dials and design, film simulations, a complete APS-C lens range | No full frame. Popular models are often back-ordered |
| [[systems/fujifilm\|Fujifilm GFX]] | Medium format | 2017 | The most affordable route into medium format | Size, cost, slower operation |
| [[systems/micro-four-thirds\|Micro Four Thirds]] | Four Thirds | 2008 | Smallest lenses, best stabilization, weather sealing, computational features | Smallest sensor here. Two stops behind full frame in low light |
| [[systems/l-mount\|L-Mount]] | Full frame, APS-C | 2014 (alliance 2018) | Three-way alliance, strong video, Sigma's full lens range | Fewer bodies for action photography |

## How to choose

**1. Start with the lenses you will need.** Look up the two or three lenses you expect to buy, in each system, and compare price and weight. This often settles the question. A wildlife photographer should compare telephotos. A traveller should compare compact zooms.

**2. Decide on sensor size.** If you want small and light, look at Fujifilm X, Micro Four Thirds, or the APS-C bodies from Sony, Canon and Nikon. If you want full frame, look at Canon, Nikon, Sony and L-Mount. See [[cameras/sensor-sizes|sensor sizes]].

**3. Handle the cameras.** Grip shape, dial layout and menu logic are personal. A camera you enjoy holding gets used.

**4. Consider what your friends use.** Borrowing lenses and getting help are worth something.

**5. Do not over-weight the current body.** Each brand leapfrogs the others every couple of years. Whoever is a little behind on one feature today will likely catch up with the next release.

## Third-party lens support

Third-party lenses from Sigma, Tamron, Viltrox and others matter because they are usually cheaper and sometimes fill gaps the camera maker has left.

| Mount | Third-party autofocus lenses |
| ----- | ---------------------------- |
| Sony E | Open since the start. The widest choice of any mount. Sony limits third-party lenses to 15 fps bursts and does not allow third-party teleconverters |
| Fujifilm X | Open. Sigma, Tamron, Viltrox and others |
| L-Mount | Open to alliance members, which include Sigma, Samyang, Sirui and Viltrox |
| Micro Four Thirds | Open standard. Sigma, Laowa and others |
| Nikon Z | Licensed selectively. Tamron zooms, Sigma APS-C primes, Viltrox primes |
| Canon RF | Most restricted. Sigma and Tamron have made licensed APS-C (RF-S) lenses since 2024. Full-frame autofocus lenses from third parties remain rare |

Policies shift. Check the current position before choosing a system on this basis.

## Mounts compared

| Mount | Flange distance | Inner diameter | Formats |
| ----- | --------------- | -------------- | ------- |
| Nikon Z | 16 mm | 55 mm | Full frame, APS-C |
| Fujifilm X | 17.7 mm | 43.5 mm | APS-C |
| Sony E | 18 mm | 46.1 mm | Full frame, APS-C |
| Micro Four Thirds | 19.25 mm | 38 mm | Four Thirds |
| Canon RF | 20 mm | 54 mm | Full frame, APS-C |
| L-Mount | 20 mm | 51.6 mm | Full frame, APS-C |
| Fujifilm G | 26.7 mm | 65 mm | Medium format |

A short flange distance and a wide opening give lens designers more freedom, especially for fast and wide lenses. It also determines what can be [[lenses/adapters|adapted]] to what.

## About the prices on these pages

Prices are US launch prices for the body only, in US dollars, unless stated. Street prices move. Older models are discounted, and import tariffs introduced in 2025 pushed many US prices above their launch figures. Treat the numbers as a guide to where a camera sits in the range, not as a quote.
