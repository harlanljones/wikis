# Canon RF

Canon is the largest camera maker by sales. Its mirrorless system, **EOS R**, uses the **RF mount**, introduced in 2018. Full-frame lenses are labelled RF and APS-C lenses RF-S. Both fit every EOS R body.

## What Canon is known for

- **Dual Pixel autofocus.** Reliable, easy to set up and strong across the whole range, including the cheapest bodies.
- **Ergonomics.** Deep grips, a fully articulating screen on nearly every body, and menus that are widely considered the easiest to learn.
- **Colour.** Pleasing skin tones straight from the camera.
- **Distinctive lenses.** Canon builds lenses nobody else does: the RF 28-70mm F2 L, the RF 100-500mm L, the RF 200-800mm, the lightweight 600mm and 800mm f/11 primes, and the 10-20mm F4 L.
- **The EF back catalogue.** Thirty years of EF DSLR lenses [[lenses/adapters|adapt]] with full function and are plentiful used.

## What to watch for

- **Third-party lenses.** RF is the most restricted mount. Sigma and Tamron make licensed APS-C lenses, but full-frame third-party autofocus lenses are rare, which keeps full-frame lens prices higher than on Sony or Nikon.
- **APS-C lens range.** Canon's own RF-S lenses are mostly slow kit zooms. The Sigma f/1.4 primes and f/2.8 zooms fill the gap.
- **Segmentation.** Cheaper bodies tend to leave out in-body stabilization and use a smaller battery.

Canon also sells Cinema EOS video cameras with the RF mount, such as the C50, C70 and C80.

## Which one

- **First camera:** R50, or R10 if you want more dials and a faster shutter.
- **Birds and wildlife on a budget:** R7 with the RF 100-400mm, or the RF 200-800mm when funds allow.
- **Cheapest good full frame:** R8, or the R8 Mark II if you want stabilization.
- **One body for everything:** R6 Mark III. A discounted R6 Mark II is nearly as capable for stills.
- **High resolution with speed:** R5 Mark II.
- **Mostly video:** R6 V or R50 V.
