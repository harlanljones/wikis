# Autofocus

Autofocus is where cameras have improved most in the last decade, and where the gap between a budget body and an expensive one is easiest to feel. A modern camera can recognise an eye, a bird or a racing car, and hold focus on it as it moves around the frame.

## How the camera finds focus

**Phase detection** compares light arriving from opposite sides of the lens. From one measurement it knows which direction to move the focus and how far. It is fast and it can predict motion.

**Contrast detection** nudges the focus back and forth and looks for the point of highest contrast. It is accurate but slow, and it "hunts" because it cannot tell which way to go.

DSLRs do phase detection with a separate sensor below the mirror. Mirrorless cameras build phase-detection pixels into the image sensor itself, an arrangement called **on-sensor phase detection** or **hybrid AF**. Every current mirrorless camera worth buying has it.

Brand names you will see:

| Term | Brand | What it is |
| ---- | ----- | ---------- |
| Dual Pixel CMOS AF | Canon | Every pixel is split in two and used for phase detection |
| Hybrid AF, Fast Hybrid AF | Sony, Nikon, Fujifilm | Phase-detection pixels spread across the sensor |
| Phase Hybrid AF | Panasonic | Phase detection, on bodies from 2023 onward |
| DFD (Depth from Defocus) | Panasonic, older bodies | Contrast-based. Fine for stills, pulses in video |
| Cross Quad Pixel AF | OM System | Each pixel split in four, sensitive in both directions |

Older Panasonic cameras (before the S5 II and G9 II in 2023) used DFD without phase detection. It is the main thing to check when buying a used Lumix body for video or action.

## Focus modes

| Mode | Canon | Nikon, Sony, Fujifilm and others | Behaviour |
| ---- | ----- | -------------------------------- | --------- |
| Single | One-Shot | AF-S | Focuses once and locks. For still subjects |
| Continuous | Servo | AF-C | Keeps refocusing while the button is held. For anything that moves |
| Automatic | AI Focus | AF-A | The camera chooses between the two |
| Manual | MF | MF | You turn the focus ring |

With modern tracking, many photographers leave the camera in continuous AF all the time.

## Focus areas

The focus area tells the camera where to look.

- **Single point / spot.** One small point that you place. Precise, for static subjects or shooting through gaps.
- **Zone / expanded area.** A cluster of points. The camera picks the nearest subject inside it.
- **Wide / whole area.** The camera searches the entire frame.
- **Tracking.** You start on a subject and the camera follows it wherever it goes.

## Subject detection

Since about 2021, cameras have used machine-learning models to recognise subjects and focus on the most useful part of them, usually the eye.

Typical detection types:

- **People:** eye, face, head, body. Works with glasses, masks, helmets and people facing away.
- **Animals:** dogs, cats, and often horses.
- **Birds:** a separate mode on most cameras. The key feature for bird photography.
- **Vehicles:** cars, motorcycles, trains, aircraft. Often targets the driver's helmet or the cockpit.
- **Insects**, on some Sony and Fujifilm bodies.

Many recent cameras add an **auto** setting that picks the subject type for you.

How well this works depends on the processor generation more than on the price tier. Canon's Dual Pixel AF II, Nikon bodies with the EXPEED 7 processor, and Sony bodies with the dedicated "AI processing unit" all perform at a high level, including their entry-level models. This is the strongest argument for buying a recent body over an older, higher-tier one.

## What the autofocus specs mean

**Number of AF points.** Marketing. Anything over a few hundred is the same in use. Coverage of the frame matters more, and nearly all mirrorless cameras cover 90 to 100 percent.

**Low-light sensitivity (for example -6.5 EV).** The dimmest light in which the camera can focus. -4 EV is roughly moonlight. Lower numbers are better. The figure is usually quoted with an f/1.2 or f/1.4 lens, so compare carefully.

**Burst rate "with AF/AE tracking".** The frames per second at which the camera still refocuses between every frame. A higher figure without tracking is less useful.

**Pre-capture (pre-burst, pre-release).** The camera buffers frames while the shutter button is half pressed and saves the half second or so *before* you fully press it. It makes unpredictable moments, such as a bird taking off, catchable.

## Getting more keepers

- **Use continuous AF with subject detection** for anything alive.
- **Raise the shutter speed.** Most "missed focus" on moving subjects is motion blur. See [[basics/shutter-speed|shutter speed]].
- **Try back-button focus.** Move focusing from the shutter button to a button under your thumb. Hold it to focus continuously, release it to lock.
- **Tune the tracking settings.** Options such as "tracking sensitivity" control how readily the camera jumps to a new subject when something passes in front.
- **Lens motors matter.** Linear and voice-coil motors keep up with fast subjects. Older screw-drive and micro-motor lenses do not.

## Manual focus aids

Mirrorless cameras make manual focus practical:

- **Focus peaking** outlines sharp edges in a bright colour.
- **Magnification** zooms the viewfinder in on the focus point.
- **Focus guides** show arrows indicating which way to turn.

These make inexpensive manual lenses and adapted vintage lenses easy to use. See [[lenses/adapters|adapters]].
