# Mirrorless vs DSLR

Both are cameras with large sensors and interchangeable lenses. The difference is how you see the picture before you take it.

## How each one works

**A DSLR** (digital single-lens reflex) has a mirror in front of the sensor. The mirror reflects light from the lens up through a prism to an optical viewfinder, so you look directly through the lens. When you press the shutter, the mirror flips up out of the way, the shutter opens, and the sensor is exposed. A separate autofocus sensor in the bottom of the camera, fed by a secondary mirror, handles focusing.

**A mirrorless camera** removes the mirror, the prism and the separate focus sensor. Light goes straight to the image sensor all the time. The sensor feeds a live image to an electronic viewfinder or the rear screen, and it does the focusing and metering itself.

## What that changes

| | DSLR | Mirrorless |
| - | ---- | ---------- |
| Viewfinder | Optical. A direct view with no lag, but it does not show exposure | Electronic. Shows the exposure, white balance and depth of field you will get |
| Autofocus | Separate module, points clustered near the centre. Different, slower system in live view | On the image sensor, across nearly the whole frame, with subject recognition |
| Focus accuracy | Lens and body can need calibration (micro-adjustment) | Measured on the sensor itself, so no calibration needed |
| Video | Limited. Autofocus in video is weak on most models | A core strength |
| Silent shooting | Mirror slap on every frame | Electronic shutter can be silent |
| Burst speed | Limited by how fast the mirror can move, 10 to 16 fps at the top end | 20 to 120 fps on recent bodies |
| Size | Deeper body to make room for the mirror | Thinner body. Lenses are often similar in size |
| Battery life | 800 to 2,000+ shots, because an optical viewfinder uses no power | 300 to 700 shots on the rating, usually more in practice |
| Lenses | Decades of used lenses at low prices | All new lens development is here |

## Where the industry is

The transition is over. Canon and Nikon have both stopped developing new DSLR bodies and lenses, and their catalogues of DSLR products shrink each year as models are discontinued. Sony ended its A-mount DSLR-style line earlier. Pentax is the only company still committed to making DSLRs.

Every significant camera released since 2021 has been mirrorless. See [[systems/dslr-systems|DSLR systems today]] for what remains on sale.

## Does a DSLR still make sense?

**Yes, in a few cases:**

- **You are on a tight budget.** A used DSLR with a good lens is the cheapest way into serious photography. A body such as a Nikon D7200 or Canon 80D with a kit lens can cost less than a new entry-level mirrorless body alone.
- **You already own DSLR lenses.** They work. They also [[lenses/adapters|adapt]] to the same brand's mirrorless bodies with full function, so nothing is stranded when you eventually switch.
- **You prefer an optical viewfinder.** Some people find it more pleasant and less tiring, particularly for long days outdoors.
- **You need extreme battery life** and cannot recharge.

**Buy mirrorless if:**

- You are buying new and starting from nothing.
- You photograph moving subjects. Modern [[cameras/autofocus|subject tracking]] is the single largest advance of the mirrorless era.
- You want to shoot video.
- You want to see the exposure before you take the picture, which makes learning much faster.

## Common misconceptions

**"Mirrorless is always smaller."** Bodies are smaller. Lenses for the same sensor size and aperture are about the same size, because that is set by physics. A full-frame mirrorless camera with a 24-70mm f/2.8 is not a small kit. For a small system, choose a smaller [[cameras/sensor-sizes|sensor]] or slower lenses.

**"DSLRs have better image quality" or the reverse.** Image quality comes from the sensor and lens. A DSLR and a mirrorless camera with the same sensor produce the same file.

**"Electronic viewfinders are laggy and grainy."** That was true in 2014. Current viewfinders refresh at 120 Hz or more and are sharp enough that most people stop noticing them. See [[cameras/viewfinders-and-screens|viewfinders and screens]].
