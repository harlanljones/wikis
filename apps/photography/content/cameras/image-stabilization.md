# Image stabilization

Stabilization counteracts the small movements of your hands so you can use slower shutter speeds without blur. It can be in the lens, in the camera body, or both.

## The two kinds

**Optical stabilization (in the lens).** A group of lens elements shifts to cancel movement. It is most effective at long focal lengths, and it steadies the view in an optical viewfinder.

| Brand | Name on the lens |
| ----- | ---------------- |
| Canon | IS |
| Nikon | VR |
| Sony | OSS |
| Fujifilm, Panasonic | OIS |
| Sigma | OS |
| Tamron | VC |
| OM System | IS |

**In-body stabilization (IBIS).** The sensor itself floats on electromagnets and moves to cancel shake. It works with every lens you mount, including old manual ones, and it corrects rotation around the lens axis, which lens stabilization cannot.

When a stabilized lens is used on a stabilized body, the two work together. Names for this include Coordinated IS (Canon), Synchro VR (Nikon), Dual I.S. (Panasonic) and Sync IS (OM System).

## What "5-axis" means

Five kinds of movement are corrected:

1. Pitch (tilting up and down)
2. Yaw (turning left and right)
3. Roll (rotating around the lens axis)
4. Horizontal shift
5. Vertical shift

Pitch and yaw matter most at normal distances. The two shift axes matter for close-up and macro work.

## What the stop rating means

A rating such as "7.5 stops" follows the CIPA test standard. It says that, in the test, photographers could shoot 7.5 stops slower than they could without stabilization and get the same sharpness.

In theory, 7.5 stops turns a 1/60 s limit into about 3 seconds. In practice:

- Ratings are best cases, measured with a specific lens under lab conditions.
- Expect about two stops less handheld in the field, and less again at long focal lengths.
- Beyond about 6 stops, the rotation of the Earth becomes a limiting factor for the gyro sensors, so ratings of 8 and 8.5 stops depend on further tricks.
- Wide lenses stabilize further than long ones.

A useful working assumption is that modern IBIS lets you handhold a normal lens at 1/4 s to 1/2 s with care, and a wide lens at around 1 second.

## What stabilization cannot do

**It does not freeze the subject.** People, animals, leaves and water still blur at slow shutter speeds. Stabilization helps with static scenes in dim light. It does not help with a child running indoors. For that you need a faster shutter speed, which means a wider aperture or higher ISO.

## Which cameras have IBIS

IBIS used to be a premium feature. It is now standard on most bodies from about $1,300 up, and on all current OM System and most Panasonic bodies. Entry-level cameras usually leave it out and rely on stabilized lenses.

| Tier | Typically |
| ---- | --------- |
| Entry-level APS-C (Canon R50 and R10, Nikon Z50 II, Sony a6400, Fujifilm X-M5 and X-T30 III) | No IBIS |
| Mid-range APS-C (Canon R7, Sony a6700, Fujifilm X-S20, X-T50 and X-T5) | IBIS |
| Entry-level full frame | Mixed. Nikon Z5 II and Canon R8 Mark II have it. The original Canon R8 does not |
| Mid-range and higher full frame | IBIS |

If you are choosing a camera without IBIS, pick stabilized lenses.

## Stabilization for video

Video is where stabilization is most visible, because every frame is seen in sequence.

- **IBIS and lens stabilization** smooth out hand tremor for static handheld shots.
- **Electronic stabilization** (Active, Dynamic, Enhanced, E-Stabilization) crops the image slightly and shifts the frame digitally. It is much more effective for walking, at the cost of a crop of 10 to 40 percent.
- **Gyro data** recorded with the footage lets software stabilize it afterward (Sony Catalyst, Gyroflow).
- A **gimbal** is still the tool for smooth moving shots.

## Other uses of a moving sensor

- **[[cameras/megapixels#Pixel shift|Pixel-shift high resolution]]**
- **Sensor cleaning** by shaking dust off
- **Automatic horizon levelling** on some bodies
- **Star tracking** on Pentax (Astrotracer), which moves the sensor to follow the stars during long exposures
- **Simulated graduated ND and Live ND filters** on OM System bodies

## When to turn it off

On a solid tripod, some systems hunt for motion that is not there and add a small blur. Many cameras detect a tripod automatically. If your tripod shots at long exposures are soft, turn stabilization off and compare.
