# Megapixels

A megapixel is one million pixels. A 24-megapixel sensor produces images about 6,000 pixels wide and 4,000 tall.

Resolution is the most advertised number on a camera and one of the least important above a certain point. For most uses, that point is around 24 megapixels.

## How much you need

| Output | Pixels needed | Megapixels |
| ------ | ------------- | ---------- |
| Instagram post | 1080 x 1350 | 1.5 |
| 4K screen, full screen | 3840 x 2160 | 8.3 |
| A4 or 8 x 10 inch print at 300 ppi | 3000 x 2400 | 7 |
| A3 or 12 x 18 inch print at 300 ppi | 5400 x 3600 | 19 |
| A2 or 16 x 24 inch print at 300 ppi | 7200 x 4800 | 35 |
| 24 x 36 inch print at 200 ppi | 7200 x 4800 | 35 |
| 40 x 60 inch print at 150 ppi | 9000 x 6000 | 54 |

Large prints are viewed from farther away, so they need fewer pixels per inch. A 24-megapixel file makes a good 24 x 36 inch print at normal viewing distance.

## Resolution grows slowly

Linear resolution, the amount of detail along one edge, goes up with the *square root* of the megapixel count. Doubling megapixels gives only 41 percent more detail in each direction.

| Megapixels | Width in pixels | Detail vs 24 MP |
| ---------- | --------------- | --------------- |
| 12 | 4,240 | 0.71x |
| 20 | 5,470 | 0.91x |
| 24 | 6,000 | 1x |
| 33 | 7,000 | 1.17x |
| 45 | 8,200 | 1.37x |
| 61 | 9,500 | 1.58x |
| 102 | 11,650 | 1.94x |

Going from 24 to 33 megapixels is barely visible. Going from 24 to 45 or 61 is a real step.

## What high resolution is good for

- **Cropping.** A 61-megapixel full-frame image cropped to the APS-C area is still 26 megapixels. This is extra reach for wildlife and a second focal length for a prime lens.
- **Very large prints** viewed up close.
- **Landscapes, architecture and studio work,** where fine texture is the point.
- **Reframing** horizontal shots as vertical, or the reverse.

## What it costs

- **File size.** A 61-megapixel raw file is 60 to 120 MB. Storage, backups and editing all slow down.
- **Technique.** High resolution shows focus errors, camera shake and subject motion that 24 megapixels hides. Use faster shutter speeds.
- **Lenses.** Cheaper lenses cannot resolve 60 megapixels across the frame.
- **Diffraction** becomes visible at wider [[basics/aperture|apertures]].
- **Speed.** More pixels take longer to read, which historically meant slower bursts and more [[cameras/shutters|rolling shutter]]. Stacked high-resolution sensors, as in the Sony a1 II, Nikon Z8, Canon R5 II and Sony a7R VI, remove most of this trade-off at a price.

## Megapixels and noise

It is often said that fewer, larger pixels are better in low light. For a finished image viewed at the same size, this is mostly false with modern sensors. A 45-megapixel image scaled down to 24 megapixels has about the same noise as a native 24-megapixel image from the same sensor size and generation.

Sensor *size* determines low-light quality. Pixel count has a minor effect, except in specialised cameras such as the 12-megapixel Sony a7S III, which is built for video.

## Pixel shift

Many cameras can take a series of frames while moving the sensor by a pixel or less, then combine them into one file with more detail and better colour. Names include Pixel Shift Multi Shooting, High Res Shot and IBIS High Res. It can produce 100 to 400 megapixel files from a static scene on a tripod. Some OM System and Panasonic bodies offer a handheld version. Moving subjects such as leaves and water cause artefacts.
